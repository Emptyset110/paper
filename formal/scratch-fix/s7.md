**Bounded state-space explorer (complete).** A normalized all-successor BFS over the reference semantics. A configuration is snapshotted to plain data and canonicalized by minimizing over all fiber permutations with atoms renamed in first-encounter order, so the normal form is a true representative of the orbit under renaming of names, keys, values, tags, and realm labels — not a heuristic hash; a self-test rebuilds a scenario state under 23 random and adversarial renamings and checks that all canonicalize alike, that a semantically different state does not, and that materialization round-trips. At every state the explorer offers *every* enabled lifecycle rule at every fiber, every eligible removal, and every orchestration input drawn from a fixed catalog of 19 component shapes together with isolated inserts, child inserts, retire, update, and a value-operation input. The working bound's non-fiber dimensions are respected exactly (2 keys, 2 values, $\le 3$ steps per shape, registration depth $\le 2$); the fiber dimension is not exhaustible at the full generator. The measured per-fiber growth of the normalized space is $38.8\times$ ($\{P,C\}$: 7,150 states at 2 fibers, 277,063 at 3), putting the full 19-shape catalog at 4 fibers past $10^9$ states, so the bound was reduced in the fiber dimension only, and the reduction is reported rather than absorbed: ten runs in all, 7,051,886 normalized states, no run reporting an internal cycle.

The suite and the coverage figures below are computed from a **six-run portfolio** whose union is the reported reachable set: the full catalog at 1 fiber with every input kind (614 states) and at 2 fibers with the update and value-operation inputs withheld (30,765), and four restricted 3-fiber generators — dependency chains (558,748), failing providers (688,484), and registration cascades (631,538 and 19,707). Five of the six are exhaustive. The sixth, the larger registration run, hit its 25-minute budget after expanding 407,815 of 631,538 discovered states; it is disclosed as truncated and paired with the exhaustive twin at the same bound, the same generator with the extra input kinds switched off. Since a truncated BFS is sound for reachability and unsound only for absence, that run contributes positive witnesses only, and **no negative claim in this section rests on it**.

Two further runs were launched at the largest bounds the machine could attempt — the full catalog at 2 fibers *with* both value operations (1.81 M states expanded) and the full catalog at 3 fibers (2.03 M) — and both hit their budgets. They are kept out of the suite's basis, because admitting truncated runs would weaken the "exhaustive, or paired with an exhaustive twin" property that the negative claims depend on, and are reported instead as corroboration. What they corroborate is the strongest saturation evidence available here: across 3.8 M additional expanded states, at the widest input alphabet and the largest fiber count attempted, the number of signatures *new* to the portfolio is **zero**. The isolated fiber-dimension probe is the same story read at letter level: lifting $\{P,C\}$ from 2 to 3 fibers grows the state space $38.8\times$ and adds 12 signatures, all of them value-operation records differing only in the multiset of lifecycle states of the fibers relying on the key — a field Appendix D deliberately folds away — so under the projection below they collapse onto one letter already witnessed at 2 fibers: **zero new letters.**

**What it found.** The portfolio's union contains **146 reachable (rule, signature) pairs**. From them the driver generates one test per pair — BFS-shortest access sequence, then the complete settle-outcome set from the reached state (the set of normalized quiescent reports over all internal schedules, the reachable event set, the reachable certificate set including *disabled* certificates, and the divergence flag) — and appends **four coda tests** for occurrences no normalized graph state can offer: the insert under a removed parent (normalization correctly erases the removed parent), the depth-2 intercept chain (interception is a derived operation no transition invokes), and the two external declared-but-uncommitted reads. So $|T_B| = 146 + 4 = 150$, replaying against the unmutated semantics 150/150 exactly. $T_B$ kills **all 27 deviants of Appendix F**, with per-deviant kill counts from 1 to 66 — but the composition matters more than the total: **without the four coda tests it kills 24 of 27.** Three mutants (`insert-allows-disposed-parent`, `intercept-outer-wins`, `proxy-skips-inactive-check`) are killed by a coda test and nothing else. The graph alone is therefore *not* a complete test basis, for a structural reason: the transition system models neither the external Def. 23 access surface nor the derived intercept operation, and both carry deviant-distinguishing content.

Auditing the existing 32-obligation suite against the same instrumentation (the proof tree runs unmodified under a resolve hook; all 32 scenarios stay green) shows it exercising **53 of the 146** signatures, leaving **93 uncovered**. Two caveats travel with those numbers. The audit runs each scenario once, under the default schedule, so 53 is a *lower* bound on the suite's coverage and 93 an upper bound on the gap. And the gap is not uniform in information: ranked by genuinely new occurrence content, the uncovered set is L-Divert's recovery content (11 signatures — every accumulator class but one, and the whole orchestrator-forced-restart column), L-Unload's re-activation chaining and $\xi$-outcome teardown (19 — the bookkeeping block D.10 flags as the interesting one), O-Retire idempotence (5 of 8), O-Remove's blocking occurrences (6 of 7), and the in-lifecycle value operation (2); the 33-signature value-operation block is high count and low information, one class under the projection below. In the other direction, **11 signatures the 32 tests do exercise lie outside the explored set** — the removed-parent insert, the depth-2 intercept, the declared-inactive read, and eight patterns needing more fibers than the portfolio affords. Neither set contains the other, which is what Appendix D's (D-C) already implies: the concrete suite is *material* for $T^\star$, not equal to it, and the uncovered list — not intuition — is the work list.

**Reconciling 146 with Appendix D's 86.** The two numbers count different units and neither corrects the other. Appendix D counts **alphabet letters**: $\Sigma_r$ is a set of occurrence letters, a window is the multiset $\kappa_r(W)$ of the letters it contains, and $92 - 6 = 86$ counts reachable letters. The explorer counts **occurrence tuples**: a signature key is the rule, the decision, and the complete field record — one point in a product of field domains. Roughly, *86 counts the alphabet and 146 counts reachable words over it*. The artifact computes the projection $\alpha$ that reads only the dimensions Appendix D letters and discards the rest; under it the 146 tuples collapse onto **74 letter-sets**, a $1.97\times$ fold, and the fold is extremely uneven. The value operation folds $35 \to 2$ ($17.5\times$): the single letter P3 has a 30-tuple fibre, because the explorer's record carries the dependent-state multiset, the state class and the value-equality bit, all of which CF2 and the witness folds remove. L-Unload folds $31 \to 27$ ($1.1\times$), and that is the instructive case: its letters are *per-item* letters plus bookkeeping blocks, so one window legitimately realizes a set of them, and 27 realizable letter-sets over 12 letters is not a discrepancy — it is what "occurrence multiset" means. **74 is an intermediate quantity, not a corrected 86**, and we do not present it as one.

The arithmetic that closes is over letters:

$$92 \;-\; 6 \;=\; 86 \;=\; \underbrace{75}_{\text{witnessed in the graph}} \;+\; \underbrace{2}_{\text{coda-covered}} \;+\; \underbrace{2}_{\text{absences Appendix D predicts}} \;+\; \underbrace{7}_{\text{generator gaps}}.$$

Of the 75 witnessed, 67 are witnessed separately and 8 only as a conflated pair. The 2 coda-covered are I3 and N4. The 2 predicted absences are B6 and T7 — and these are Appendix D's reachability analysis being *confirmed*, not gaps: B6 ($\mathsf{installed\mbox{-}only\mbox{-}provided}$) is what the `satisfied-by-installed-provider` deviant does and no baseline $\sigma_\gamma$ produces, and T7's absence as an in-lifecycle *step* is D.14's A4 argument read at step level. The 7 remaining are generator gaps, each with an identified cause and a known fix: M2 (the explorer offers a removal only where it is eligible, so the $\tau = \bot$ refusal is never an offered action), X9 (no catalog shape registers a child and then raises), S4 (the generator offers one isolate context and never nests a second), N1–N3 (interception has no transition at all), and A3 (the child-insert probe shape has no read step). None is a disagreement about the semantics; **no letter is contradicted anywhere**.

What stays unreconciled, stated plainly: those 7 letters; the 8 letters reached but not separated by the explorer's encoding (D3 from D4 and V3 from V4, because the model's target computation returns $\bot$ for retirement and for provider withdrawal alike; A5 from A6, because the access walk does not record whether it died at the root or at a realm boundary; S2 from S3, because one isolate context makes distinct and joined realms the same signature); and — symmetrically, a gap in the *hand* analysis rather than the machine's — **6 explorer signatures with no Appendix D letter at all**: L-Divert under an orchestrator-forced restart, which D.9 letters only at L-Leave (V5) and D.7 does not letter at its own rule. Finally, $\alpha$ is hand-written; it encodes one reading of the letter tables and is not itself verified beyond transcription.

**The six negative claims, stratified by the strength of their evidence.** All six are absent from the reachable set, but three grades of evidence hide behind that sentence and the paper states which is which. **D2, V2 and A7 are genuine negative search results**: the classifier branches that would emit them are live code — the changed-provider trigger at L-Divert and at L-Leave, the withdrawn-binding outcome in the access walk — and 7.05 M states never reach them. That is the machine-checked form of D.16's A10 argument and of Thm. 63's content. **I5 and D6 are absent because the model shares Appendix D's own assumption**: names are system-drawn, so no freshness field exists, and the reference L-Divert has only an abort branch. Their absence is agreement, not independent corroboration, and we do not claim it as such. **A4 is realizable after all, and the tables' claim is narrowed accordingly** (next paragraph).

**Correction to a negative claim: A4 holds only for in-lifecycle windows.** D.14 excluded A4 — the Algorithm-6 inactive-access decided at the accessing fiber itself — on the argument that a fiber's own committed view is total on its declared keys while it runs, so no *step* can present the occurrence. The exploration confirms exactly that statement: no L-Iter step class and no in-graph read of that class occurs anywhere in 7.05 M states. But the argument quantifies over steps, while the occurrence is a property of Algorithm 6, which the *environment* can also invoke: an external Def. 23 read on a fiber that declares the key and holds no committed view walks from that fiber and decides there. Two of the explorer's coda tests do it, the 32-test suite's `C5.3 declared-inactive-access` does it, and it is not idle — that coda test is the sole killer of the `proxy-skips-inactive-check` deviant. So A4 is unrealizable as an in-lifecycle window and realizable as an external access; D.14 and D.15 now say so. This is a scope refinement, not a refutation, and it is the one place where execution qualified a hand claim rather than confirming it. Its bookkeeping consequence is recorded, not hidden: on the strict reading that the coeffect alphabets letter *operations* the environment may invoke, A4 counts as reachable and the summary would read 87 reachable and 5 unrealizable; the tables keep 86/6, excluding A4 as a lifecycle-window occurrence, and note that the experiment which would witness it already exists in both suites. The characterization is unaffected either way — the letter is covered by a test in both readings — but the count is a judgement call and we flag it as one.

**Correction C1's repair, checked twice.** The explorer reproduces C1 in five lines: in D.7's uncorrected script, `insert(root, Q2)` is refused, because `retire` does not deregister Q and O-Insert's disjointness premise quantifies over every registered non-removed fiber — the refusal itself being a real, tested occurrence (it is the sole killer of the `insert-allows-duplicate-provider` deviant). The artifact adds that *one* obvious repair also fails: settling after retiring **both** Q and G disposes the consumer C along with its registering parent, leaving no fiber whose target can turn. The corrected script of D.7 and E.LD.D3 is not that repair — it settles after retiring Q *only*, and defers `retire(G)` until after Q2's insert, so that C survives the middle settle as a pending, unretired fiber and G is still Active to re-enter teardown afterwards. We checked it directly against the explorer's own instrumented semantics: the insert is accepted, and on a fair schedule in which Q2 activates before G's teardown runs, C re-begins against Q2 and an L-Divert fires at C while G's registration inverse retires it — certificate `L-Divert fired {trigger:"target-bot", acc:["track"]}`, with Q2 Active at that window, so the target is $\bot$ by retirement, which is D3. Two caveats are part of the result. The occurrence is *schedule-dependent*, as D.7 says: the model's default schedule takes the other branch, where G tears down before Q2 activates and C never re-begins; the verdict for this experiment is therefore the exhaustive outcome set of obligation (D-E), not a single run. And the explorer's certificates cannot separate D3 from D4, both presenting as a $\bot$-target: the attribution to D3 rests on the derivation (E.LD.D3), not on the certificate string.
