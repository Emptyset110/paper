## 6. The Characterization Theorem

**Theorem 2 (finite characterization).** For every $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$:

$$\mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S \iff \mathbb I \models T^\star,$$

on the orchestration and component language of §3 — where, by Theorem 5, the termination domain covers the whole language, the acyclicity proviso of [SZC26] Thm. 66 being discharged rather than assumed — and where observation equivalence is taken in the certificate vocabulary of Definition 1.

*(Vocabulary proviso, from §6.3.)* The statement is for the certificate-instrumented observation vocabulary; by Proposition 1's consequences, in the certificate-erased vocabulary both sides must be read on the observation-relevant quotient of the decision tables, and the biconditional persists in that quotiented form because masked entries perturb neither side.

The proof decomposes into five results — factorization (§6.1), transport (§6.2), reflection (§6.3), safety (§6.4), termination (§6.5) — assembled in §6.6. Each is proved in full; the per-rule enumeration behind the factorization is Appendix C.

### 6.1 Factorization: the calculus is clause-factored

**Theorem 3 ($\mathbb S \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$).** Each rule's applicability is a function of its window's occurrence multiset; each write is the lift of per-occurrence updates under the row's declared folds; emissions are per-occurrence; equivariance and framing hold. In full: $\mathbb S$ satisfies CF1–CF7.

The load is CF3, discharged rule-by-rule against Table 1 in Appendix C.1–C.10 and, for the operation content of iterator landings, in C.11. For each rule, Appendix C gives: **(W)** the window read off the row and its premises; **(K)** the occurrence decomposition $\kappa_r$ against the alphabet of Appendix D; **(G)** the proof that each premise is a Boolean function of the occurrence support; **(U)** the proof that the row's write is $\mathrm{lift}_r$ of per-occurrence updates, with $\widehat U_r$ enumerated; **(E)** emissions; **(Q)** equivariance and frame, by Lemmas 2–3; **(C)** the no-cardinality check for the row — an explicit inventory of every symbol the row and its premises contain, verifying that none is an arithmetic or cardinality read. The check (C) is done separately for all ten rows: *Table 1 contains no arithmetic*, and this last observation is the exact property Theorem 1's counterexample lacks. Here we assemble the seven obligations.

*Proof.* We discharge the obligations of Definition 4.

**CF1** (faithful abstraction, complete tagging). Take $A = \mathrm{id} : \Gamma_{\mathbb S} \to \Gamma_{\mathbb S}$. It is total, maps the initial (empty) registry to itself, and preserves every report field on the nose. Tagging: every transition of $\mathbb S$ *is* an application of exactly one of the ten rules at one fiber (Def. 53: $\mathrm{step}^t = r(n)$), so the tag is the step's own rule and there are no untagged transitions at all — in particular none that change an abstract field or emit an event.

**CF2** (equivariance). Lemma 2, with the reserved constants listed in §2.1.

**CF3** (signature factorization). Appendix C.1–C.11: for each rule, enablement and refusal are the guard formula of Definition 8(1) evaluated on the occurrence support — proved per rule in the (G) items; the abstract update and events are $\mathrm{lift}_r$ of the per-occurrence decisions — proved in the (U)/(E) items, with $\widehat U_r$ enumerated; and the table $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ so defined inspects nothing but $\Sigma_r$-data: the (C) items verify, row by row, that no premise or write reads a registry cardinality, an allocation order, an unnormalized spelling, or a count of witnesses. The finiteness of each $\Sigma_r$ is **(D-A)** (proved per alphabet in Appendix D).

**CF4** (instrumentation faithfulness). $\mathbb S$ emits its own certificate at each step: $\mathsf{cert}(r, \widehat s, \widehat a)$ with $\widehat s$ the $\kappa_r$-image of the very window the premises just read and $\widehat a$ the normalized update the step performs (or $\mathsf{disabled}$ where an attempted orchestration input fails). Faithfulness is definitional — the certificate is computed from the same reads and writes the step makes, synchronously at the transition (Def. 53's step indexing leaves no interleaving point between the premise evaluation and the edit). The *reflection property* itself is proved for arbitrary $\mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$-members in §6.3 and hence in particular for $\mathbb S$.

**CF5** (canonical reachability). By Appendix D's tables: **(D-C)** every $s \in \Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$ carries a canonical script $\rho_{r,s}$ reaching, from the empty registry, a pointed configuration whose $\kappa_r$-image contains $s$, with a renaming-invariant construction. (Appendix D constructs the scripts; invariance under renaming follows from Lemma 2 applied to the script's run.)

**CF6** (settling). Fairness is vacuous for the specification read as the full nondeterministic step relation (every maximal sequence is considered; "the steps are any sequence of rule applications, so a theorem proved over all such sequences holds for every scheduling policy", [SZC26] p. 33). That every maximal lifecycle sequence from a reachable state terminates in a quiescent state — *without* an acyclicity side condition, hence for every state the orchestration language can produce — is Theorem 5 below. Divergence is therefore never an outcome of $\mathbb S$ on the test language, and the $\mathsf{Diverge}$ constructor is emitted vacuously-correctly.

**CF7** (witness fidelity). For $\mathbb S$, the effect functions are the witnessed iterators of Def. 51/eq. 49: the witness condition $g(\delta) \simeq \gamma$ is a *premise* of the type $\mathfrak E^{\mathrm{iter}*}_\Gamma$ itself, so the inverses supplied by test components implement the witnessed inverse relation by typing; their composition and invocation are the $g \circ h$ and $\Psi = g$ of Appendix C.5/C.10. Refusals: the orchestration rules' premises are exactly the refusal conditions of Definition 2's script semantics, refused with an observable refusal event and log entry (Appendix C's (E) items). $\square$

### 6.2 Transport

The lemma says exactly how far membership in $\mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ pins an implementation's behavior across configurations the suite never visited: per-occurrence decisions transport along $\kappa$-equality; whole-rule results transport along $\kappa$-equality *plus frame correspondence*. Remark 1 explains why the second half needs the extra hypothesis — accumulator *order* lives in the frame, and a whole-decision transport across order-differing, multiset-equal windows is false.

**Lemma 4 (transport).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ with abstraction $A$, let $r \in \mathrm{Rules}$, and let $u, u' \in \mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I)$ with acting fibers $n, n'$. Write $W = W_r(A(u), n)$ and $W' = W_r(A(u'), n')$. Suppose there is a sort-preserving bijection $\chi$ with

$$\kappa_r(\chi \cdot W) = \kappa_r(W') \quad \text{as multisets over } \Sigma_r.$$

(By Lemma 1 the left side equals $\kappa_r(W)$, so the hypothesis is equivalently the bare multiset equality $\kappa_r(W) = \kappa_r(W')$; the bijection $\chi$ is nevertheless part of the data because the conclusion's correspondences are stated along it.) Then:

1. *(Enablement and refusal.)* $\mathbb I$'s $r$-tagged transition is enabled at $(u,n)$ iff at $(u',n')$, and when refused, refused with the same reason $\varrho$.
2. *(Per-occurrence updates and emissions.)* For every multiset bijection $\beta : \kappa_r(W) \to \kappa_r(W')$ matching equal occurrences, and every occurrence $s \in \kappa_r(W)$, the per-occurrence decision of $\mathbb I$ at $s$ in $W$ equals its decision at $\beta(s)$ in $W'$: same $\mathrm{Decision}_r$-value, hence the same normalized update constructor and the same emission constructors, with payloads corresponding under $\chi$'s equality-pattern action.
3. *(Whole-rule results.)* If moreover $\chi$ and some $\beta$ carry $W$'s combination frame to $W'$'s — same index-set correspondence and same order data (iterator position; accumulator composition order) — then the full abstract successor, certificate, and emitted word at $(u,n)$ are carried by $\chi$ to those at $(u',n')$, up to $\simeq/\approx$.

*Proof.* The quantification, stated pedantically once: the lemma quantifies over *all* pairs of reachable implementation configurations and acting fibers with $\kappa$-equal windows; $\chi$ acts on the abstract window $W$ (a tuple of abstract fields plus frame — Definition 5) by its sortwise action on every carried name, key, value, and tag; nothing is assumed about the relation between $u$ and $u'$ as implementation states beyond reachability — that is the point.

(1) By CF3, $\mathbb I$'s enablement at $(u,n)$ is $\mathrm{lift}_r$'s guard formula (Definition 8(1)) evaluated on the family $\{f_r^{\mathbb I}(s)\}_{s \in \kappa_r(W)}$. The guard formula is a fixed Boolean combination depending only on which occurrence classes are present and their per-class decisions (§5.1, "guards factor through the support"). The multiset equality is used here through its consequence $\mathrm{supp}(\kappa_r(W)) = \mathrm{supp}(\kappa_r(W'))$: equal multisets have equal supports, and $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ — being a *function* on $\Sigma_r$, which is what CF3's factorization demands — assigns each class the same decision in both windows. Hence the two guard evaluations are literally the same Boolean computation on the same inputs, and agree; likewise the leftmost-failing-premise reason. Note what is *not* used: no relation between the concrete sizes, spellings, or layouts of $u$ and $u'$ — CF3's prohibition on inspecting registry cardinality, allocation order, unnormalized spelling, or the number of irrelevant witnesses is exactly what makes the occurrence multiset the whole input.

(2) Let $\beta$ be any multiset bijection matching equal occurrences (one exists by the hypothesis; there may be several when a class has multiplicity $> 1$, and the conclusion holds for each). For $s \in \kappa_r(W)$ we have $\beta(s) = s$ *as elements of $\Sigma_r$* (occurrences are normalized data; $\beta$ matches equal ones), so $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s) = f_r^{\mathbb I}(\beta(s))$ holds by reflexivity — the entire content of clause (2) is that $\mathbb I$ consults nothing else, which is CF3, plus the translation of the normalized payloads back to concrete identities: the update constructor's payload is an equality pattern over the occurrence's read identities, and $\chi$ carries $W$'s identities to $W'$'s matching ones by the hypothesis that $\chi\cdot W$ and $W'$ have equal $\kappa$-images at matched occurrences (Lemma 1(3): equality patterns determine, and are determined by, the identity constellation up to bijection). CF2 enters here: $\mathbb I$'s *concrete* per-occurrence behavior at $(u',n')$ is the $\chi$-image of its behavior at $(u,n)$ because renaming before the transition renames its result and events in the same way — without CF2, equal decisions on normalized occurrences could still be realized by non-corresponding concrete writes (e.g. an implementation writing a fixed hard-coded key when the pattern says "the read key"); equivariance excludes precisely that.

(3) By CF3, the full result at each window is $\mathrm{lift}_r$(frame, per-occurrence decisions) — with the *same, specified* $\mathrm{lift}_r$ at both windows (CF3 fixes the reconstruction operator; it is not per-implementation data). By (2) the decision families correspond under $(\chi,\beta)$; by the added hypothesis the frames correspond under the same pair. $\mathrm{lift}_r$'s three clauses are each invariant under such a correspondence: the guard formula by (1); the finite-map product because $\beta$ restricted to per-key occurrences is induced by $\chi_K$ on the index sets and the per-key supports are disjoint singletons; the order-sensitive folds because the order data correspond by hypothesis and the fold is the same binary operator applied in the corresponding order; the emission concatenation likewise. The certificate is $(r, \widehat s, \widehat a)$ with both components $\kappa$-normalized, hence *equal* (not just corresponding) at the two windows by Lemma 1 — which is the transport fact the verdicts rely on. The successor abstractions agree up to $\simeq/\approx$ because the assembled updates are equal as edits of corresponding fields (Lemma 55's conclusion paragraph gives the same-shape statement for $\mathbb S$; here it holds for $\mathbb I$'s *abstract image* by CF1's field preservation: $A$ carries the performed abstract update to the report-visible fields). $\square$

**Remark 2 (how the lemma is consumed).** In the safety proof (§6.4) the lemma is applied in the degenerate but crucial instance $W' = $ the window of the *specification* state matched to $A(u)$ by the induction hypothesis, $\chi$ the accumulated renaming, $\beta$ the identity on matched occurrences — there the frames correspond automatically (same abstract window up to $\simeq/\approx$, Lemma 1), so clause (3) applies and gives whole-rule agreement between $\mathbb I$'s step and $\mathbb S$'s. The genuinely two-implementation-states instance (clause (2) without (3)) is what licenses the canonical scripts of Appendix D to *stand for* every $\kappa$-equal configuration: the suite pins $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ at the canonical window, and transport carries the per-occurrence pin everywhere; the frame data at the deployment window is then handled by lift — CF3 extends the checked binary composition operator to accumulators of arbitrary finite length, an induction the lift makes and the test alone does not — checked once, at the accumulator-order and product tests of §5.4, not re-derived per configuration.
