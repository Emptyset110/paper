### 6.3 Reflection, and the necessity of certificates

Throughout this subsection fix $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$, a rule $r$, and a reachable signature $s \in \Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$ with its canonical experiment $(\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s}) \in T^\star$. Recall (§5.4) that $V_{r,s}$ requires equality, up to $\equiv$, of $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s})$ with the complete specification outcome set — equivalently: enablement or refusal equal to $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$; an equal normalized certificate; the prescribed ordinary event word; the prescribed normalized successor report after settling; termination whenever the specification terminates. Of Appendix D's obligations we use: **(D-C)** $\rho_{r,s}$'s unique-up-to-$\equiv$ specification run reaches, after its prefix $\rho_0$, a pointed configuration $\gamma^\star$ with $s \in \kappa_r(W_r(\gamma^\star, n^\star))$, and its final action attempts $r$ there (directly for orchestration rules; by one lifecycle step followed by settling for lifecycle rules); **(D-E)** the specification outcome set of $\rho_{r,s}$ is computed by exhaustive exploration of every fair choice, and is what $V_{r,s}$ compares against; **(D-F)** every specification schedule of $\rho_{r,s}$ presents the same normalized signature $s$ at the attempt point (attempt-point determinacy).

**Lemma 5 (reflection).** If $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s) \neq f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$, then $\mathbb I \not\models (\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s})$.

*Proof.* Suppose toward a contradiction that $\mathbb I \models (\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s})$, i.e. every run of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho_{r,s}$ is explored, non-divergent where the specification terminates, and $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s}) \equiv \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho_{r,s})$.

**Step 0 (a run of $\mathbb I$ reaches the attempt point).** The specification outcome set contains an outcome whose certificate word passes through the attempt point with signature $s$ (D-C). Outcome-set equality in the direction $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S} \subseteq \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}$ (up to $\equiv$) yields a run $\pi$ of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho_{r,s}$ whose outcome $\equiv$-matches it; fix the sort-preserving bijection $\chi$ realizing the match. Along $\pi$, the certificate letters equal (after normalization, on which $\chi$ acts trivially — Lemma 1) the specification run's certificates; by CF4's faithfulness each certificate of $\mathbb I$ truthfully reports the occurrence its step consulted and the abstract update it performed, synchronously at the transition boundary — **[CF4 used]** — so at the position matching the attempt point, $\mathbb I$'s configuration $u^\star$ has $A(u^\star)$ presenting the signature $s$ at the acting fiber $\chi^{-1}(n^\star)$: the preceding certificates pin every abstract update made so far, and CF1's field preservation plus the report-free determinism of the abstract edit sequence give $A(u^\star) \simeq \chi^{-1}\cdot\gamma^\star$ (formally: induct along the prefix; at each step the certificate equality gives equal normalized $(\widehat s, \widehat a)$, and equal normalized updates applied to $\simeq$-related abstract states yield $\simeq$-related successors, Lemma 55's conclusion paragraph). By Lemma 1, $s \in \kappa_r(W_r(A(u^\star), \chi^{-1}n^\star))$.

**Step 1 (the deviant entry is used).** At $u^\star$ the script's final action attempts $r$. By CF3, $\mathbb I$'s decision there applies $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ pointwise on $\kappa_r(W_r(A(u^\star), \chi^{-1}n^\star))$ — in particular at $s$ — and combines with $\mathrm{lift}_r$. We case on the component of $\mathrm{Decision}_r$ at which $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s)$ and $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ differ. The cases are exhaustive over $\mathrm{Decision}_r$'s structure (Definition 7): enablement polarity; refusal reason; update constructor; emission constructors. Termination is handled in case (e).

**Case (a): enablement differs — $\mathbb S$ fires, $\mathbb I$ refuses (or is silent).** Every specification outcome of $\rho_{r,s}$ contains, at the attempt point, the certificate $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb S})$ with $\widehat a^{\mathbb S}$ the update component of $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ — *every* outcome, by attempt-point determinacy (D-F) and because $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ is a function: no specification schedule can present a different $\widehat a$ at $\widehat s = s$. Now consider $\mathbb I$'s outcomes through $u^\star$ (nonempty by Step 0). If $r$ is an orchestration rule: $\mathbb I$ refuses the input, emitting a refusal event and logging it (CF4's synchrony makes the refusal, not some later compensation, the recorded observation — **[CF4 used]**); its outcome is $\mathsf{Refused}(\cdots)$ or contains the refusal letter, while no matching specification outcome refuses there (the specification premise is true at $s$ — that is what $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s) = \mathsf{fire}$ means, by Theorem 3's CF3 for $\mathbb S$); outcome unmatched. If $r$ is a lifecycle rule: $\mathbb I$ does not offer the $r$-step at $u^\star$; its runs from $u^\star$ either take other enabled steps or settle. In either case the outcome's word lacks the letter $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb S})$ at any position compatible with the matched prefix: a later occurrence of that certificate would require a later configuration presenting $s$ at the same fiber with $\mathbb I$ firing — but $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s)$ refuses *wherever* $s$ is presented (CF3: the table is a function of the occurrence alone; this is Transport, Lemma 4(1), doing its exact job). Since $\equiv$ preserves letter order and compares certificates after normalization (Definition 1's convention), an $\mathbb I$-outcome missing a certificate letter that every specification outcome contains is matched by no specification outcome. Hence $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I} \not\equiv \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}$ — contradiction. The symmetric sub-case ($\mathbb S$ refuses, $\mathbb I$ fires) is written out only in its one asymmetric point: $\mathbb I$'s outcome now *contains* a certificate $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \cdot)$ (or, for orchestration rules, lacks the refusal letter and log entry that every specification outcome carries — "no hidden acceptance") that no specification outcome contains at $\widehat s = s$; unmatched for the same order-preservation reason.

**Case (b): both fire, the update differs — $\widehat a^{\mathbb I} \neq \widehat a^{\mathbb S}$ at $s$.** $\mathbb I$'s step at the attempt point emits $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb I})$, truthfully by CF4 — **[CF4 used, and this is its load-bearing use: without faithfulness the implementation could perform the deviant update while certifying the specified one, and Proposition 1 shows the event/report channels alone do not always catch it]**. As in case (a), *every* specification outcome's certificate at $\widehat s = s$ is $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb S})$, because $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ is a function and certificates are normalized (Lemma 1 kills renaming as an escape: $\widehat a$ is $\chi$-invariant). An outcome containing $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb I})$ with $\widehat a^{\mathbb I} \neq \widehat a^{\mathbb S}$ is therefore matched by no specification outcome. Contradiction.

**Case (c): both fire, updates agree, the emission differs** — a wrong, missing, extra, or reordered ordinary-event contribution (e.g. $\mathsf{app}$/$\mathsf{inv}$ letters, their order, a wrong $\mathsf{rd}$ payload class). Within one rule application the emission is a function of the decision family and frame ($\mathrm{lift}$ clause (3)); the deviation therefore appears in the emitted subword of the attempt step. Ordinary event order is preserved *exactly* by $\equiv$ (Definition 1, with the stated intent: LIFO recovery and provider-before-consumer ordering are temporal claims and must not disappear inside a state equivalence). Could the deviant word coincide with a *different* specification schedule's word? Across schedules the specification varies only the interleaving of steps of distinct fibers, not the within-step contribution; and by **(D-G)** the canonical script for an order-sensitive aspect pins a schedule-invariant distinguishing subword (single-fiber forcing, as in Appendix D's S-Unl-1, where all three $\mathsf{inv}$ letters belong to one fiber's one L-Unload step). Hence the deviant outcome's word differs from every specification outcome's word at the first deviant letter position within that subword; unmatched. Contradiction.

**Case (d): refusal-reason difference (orchestration rules, both refuse).** The refused-input log is part of the quiescent report; CF1 preserves report fields and CF4 makes the log truthful — **[CF4 used]**. Two refusals with different reasons yield $\equiv$-different reports (reasons are normalized constants); every specification outcome carries the reason of $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$'s leftmost failing premise (Definition 8(1)); unmatched. Contradiction.

**Case (e): termination.** If, at or after the attempt, $\mathbb I$'s settle diverges while the specification's canonical run terminates (it always does — Theorem 5), then some run of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho_{r,s}$ yields $\mathsf{Diverge}$ or exceeds the scenario's proved transition bound, and Definition 2's passing judgment makes the test fail directly: a timeout or $\mathsf{Diverge}$ is a failure whenever the specification's canonical run terminates. Contradiction.

The five cases cover every component in which the two $\mathrm{Decision}_r$ values can differ, so the assumption falls. $\square$

**Proposition 1 (masking: reflection is *not* available in the certificate-free vocabulary).** Let $\mathrm{Out}^-$ denote outcomes with all certificate letters erased (the raw vocabulary: ordinary events, refusals, reports, termination). There exist $r$, $s \in \Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$, and a table $f^\sharp$ with $f^\sharp(s) \neq f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ such that the implementation $\mathbb I^\sharp$ obtained from $\mathbb S$ by replacing $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ with $f^\sharp(s)$ (all else unchanged) satisfies: for *every* script $\rho$, $\mathrm{Out}^-_{\mathbb I^\sharp}(\rho) \equiv \mathrm{Out}^-_{\mathbb S}(\rho)$. Concretely: $r = $ L-Unload, $s$ the signature with scalar atoms $\langle \theta{=}\mathsf{Unloading},\ \mathrm{cls}(\zeta) = \bot,\ \tau_n = \top,\ \text{post-target} = \bot \rangle$, no reliance witness, and an accumulator item of class $\mathsf{provide}^{-1}$; $f^\sharp(s)$ replaces that item's $\mathsf{apply\mbox{-}inverse}(\mathsf{provide}^{-1})$ by $\mathsf{skip}$.

*Proof.* $\mathbb I^\sharp$ deviates only in the store write of the retirement-unload's provide-inverse: after its L-Unload at a retired fiber $n$, the residue $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_n)$ survives where the specification has $\sigma_n \smallsetminus k$. We show no certificate-free observation, on any script, ever differs.

(i) *Events:* an item of class $\mathsf{provide}^{-1}$ emits no ordinary letter (Appendix C.10(E) — $\mathcal E$ has no un-provide letter), so the emitted words of the deviant and specified steps are equal.

(ii) *Reports:* the store report is $\mathrm{st}(k) = \sigma_\gamma(k)$, and $\sigma_\gamma$ unions the tables of *Active* fibers only (eq. 40); after the unload $n$ is $\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$, so $\sigma_n$ — with or without residue — contributes nothing; lifecycle classes and outcomes are untouched.

(iii) *Future behavior:* the residue could matter only through a later read of $\sigma_n$. By Lemma 55's premise inventory, no premise of any rule reads $\sigma_m(k)$ of a non-Active $m$ (premises reach $\sigma$ only through $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_\gamma)$ and $\mathrm{provider}$); an effect function's reads are confined by Def. 48(2) to tables *through the fiber's own declared window*, again via the Active union or the committed view (Alg. 6 reads views, not stores). The one path back would be a re-activation of $n$, whose effects run against $\sigma_n$; but $s$ has $\tau_n = \top$, $\tau$ is monotone (Lemma 54(5): written only at $\top$), so $\mathrm{target}_n = \bot$ forever (Def. 46) and L-Begin never fires at $n$ again. O-Remove's premises read $\tau_n$, $\theta_n$, and parenthood — not $\sigma_n$ — and removal erases the residue. Hence by induction along any continuation, every reachable pair of states (deviant vs. specified) differs only in the dead field $\sigma_n$ of the permanently retired, non-Active $n$, which no premise, event, or report ever reads.

(iv) *Termination:* the deviant table changes no guard, so the step relation's shape — and Theorem 5's argument — is unchanged.

So all certificate-free outcome sets agree. $\square$

**Consequences — stated prominently, and carried to the main theorem.**

1. **The theorem's vocabulary is the certificate-instrumented one.** Lemma 5, and therefore Theorem 2, hold for the observation vocabulary of Definition 1, in which certificates are letters of $w$ and $V_{r,s}$ compares them. This is not a technicality: by Proposition 1 the biconditional in the certificate-*free* vocabulary would require *weakening both sides the same way* — and it does hold there in weakened form: the deviant $\mathbb I^\sharp$ of the proposition also *conforms* in the certificate-free reading of $\mathbf{conf}$ (its outcome sets match $\mathbb S$'s on every script), so it is a counterexample to neither direction. The calculus is *self-healing* at the masked entries: a decision difference there is unobservable because it is semantically inert. What is genuinely lost without certificates is only the slogan "passing determines the table" (Lemma 6): table equality holds on the *observation-relevant quotient* of $\mathrm{Decision}_r$, not on the nose. This also explains an empirical fact we had met earlier: mutation analysis of the concrete suite found exactly this aspect unkillable.
2. **Certificate-adequacy obligation (sharpening CF4).** For Lemma 5's case (b) to have force, the certificate component $\widehat a$ must actually expose the per-occurrence update at every entry — including $\sigma$-writes on fibers that are not Active at report time (the masked entries above). CF4 must therefore be read (and audited) as: *the adapter's $\widehat a$ ranges over all of $\widehat U_r$, including update aspects invisible to events and reports*. An adapter that derives certificates from the event/report stream alone cannot discharge CF4 at the masked entries; Proposition 1 is the proof. Definition 4's CF4 states this clause explicitly.
