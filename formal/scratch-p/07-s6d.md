### 6.5 Termination, and progress without acyclicity

Safety (§6.4) matches every *finite* behavior. This subsection rules out the remaining escape: an implementation that agrees on all finite prefixes but settles forever (or stops settling early), and — on the specification side — establishes that $\mathbb S$ itself never diverges on the test language, *including* the $\prec$-cyclic scenarios outside Thm. 66's hypotheses.

**Interpretive convention (CF6, exact settling).** We read CF6's clause "settle terminates exactly when a quiescent state is reached" as the conjunction: (i) if a settle's run reaches a state $u$ with $A(u)$ quiescent (eq. 45), the settle returns at $u$ after finitely many further (necessarily untagged, abstractly irrelevant) steps; (ii) a settle does not return at any $u$ with $A(u)$ not quiescent. This is the only reading under which the clause is checkable against the abstraction, and it is logged as an interpretation, not a change.

**Lemma 7 (stutter exclusion).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ with $\mathbb I \models T^\star$, and let a fair settle of $\mathbb I$ produce an infinite run $u_0 \to u_1 \to \cdots$. Then the run contains infinitely many *tagged* steps.

*Proof.* Suppose only finitely many steps are tagged; let $u_N$ follow the last one. Every step after $N$ is untagged, hence (CF1's tagging totality: there are no untagged transitions that change an abstract field or emit an event) changes no abstract field: $A(u_t)$ is constant $= A(u_N)$ for $t \ge N$ up to the identity on abstract fields. Two cases on $A(u_N)$. If $A(u_N)$ is quiescent: by CF6(i) the settle returns after finitely many further steps — contradicting infinitude of the run *as a settle run*. If $A(u_N)$ is not quiescent: since steps $\le N$ form a finite tagged execution, Theorem 4 matches it by a specification execution with endpoint $\gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}A(u_N)$, reachable and non-quiescent; by Theorem 5(1) below (no deadlock, general form) some lifecycle rule is enabled at $\gamma$, hence — by the type-B argument of Theorem 4 — offered by $\mathbb I$ at $u_N$ and at every later $u_t$ (the abstract state, hence the guard evaluation, is constant along the untagged tail). CF6's fairness among enabled lifecycle rules then forces the offer to be taken after finitely many steps — a tagged step after $N$. Contradiction. $\square$

**Two auxiliary invariants.** Both are stated for $\mathbb S$; through CF1 and Theorem 4 they transfer to $A$-images of matched executions. "Reachable" means reached from the empty registry by any finite step sequence (orchestration and lifecycle, Def. 53), which by Thm. 59 is well formed (Def. 58).

**Lemma 8 ($\omega$-provenance).** At every reachable $\gamma$: for all $n$ with $\mathrm{installed}_n(\gamma)$ and all $k \in d_n$, the resolver $m := \omega_n(k)$ satisfies $m \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$, $\mathrm{installed}_m(\gamma)$, and $k \in p_m$.

*Proof.* The first two conjuncts are Def. 58(3)–(4), maintained by Thm. 59. The third, by induction along the history. It holds vacuously at $\gamma^0$. Steps preserving it: $\omega_n$ comes into existence only at an L-Begin of $n$ (Lemma 54(2)), which writes $\omega_n = \mathrm{target}_n(\gamma^t) \neq \bot$, whose value at $k$ is $\mathrm{provider}_k(\gamma^t)$ — an Active fiber $m$ with $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$ (eq. 40 and Def. 43's $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_n) \subseteq p_n$); $p_m$ is never written after $m$'s entry (Lemma 54(5)), so the conjunct persists while $\omega_n$ does; $\omega_n$ is constant throughout the episode (Lemma 54(2)) and ceases at L-Unload, after which the claim for $n$ is vacuous until the next L-Begin; O-Remove cannot remove $m$ while $n$ is installed (its premise $\theta_m = \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$ plus Def. 58(4): $\mathrm{installed}_n \wedge \omega_n(k) = m \Rightarrow \mathrm{installed}_m$, so the premise fails); no other step touches any field named. $\square$

**Lemma 9 (frozen cycle).** Let $\gamma$ be reachable and let $C = \{n_1, \dots, n_c\}$ ($c \ge 1$, indices mod $c$) be a $\prec$-cycle within $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$: $n_i \prec n_{i+1}$ for all $i$, i.e. (Def. 65) there are keys $k_{i+1} \in p_{n_i} \cap d_{n_{i+1}}$. Then at $\gamma$: no member of $C$ is installed, and every member has $\mathrm{target}_{n_i}(\gamma) = \bot$; consequently no lifecycle rule applies at any member (L-Begin's premise $\mathrm{target} \neq \bot$ fails; every other lifecycle rule's premise $\theta \neq \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$ fails), and each member satisfies its $\mathrm{quiet}$ clause (eq. 45, first line, via $\mathrm{target} = \bot$).

*Proof.* *Self-loops ($c = 1$; $k \in p_n \cap d_n$) first, as the base the general propagation cannot handle.* Suppose $n$ is installed at some reachable state; by Lemma 54(4) there is a first step in its history making it installed, an L-Begin of $n$ at some $\gamma^t$, whose premise gives $\gamma^t \vDash d_n$, so $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_{\gamma^t})$: some Active $m$ has $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$. Since $k \in p_n$ and both $m, n \in \mathrm{dom}(F_{\gamma^t})$, Def. 58(2) forces $m = n$; but $\theta_n = \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ at its own L-Begin, not Active. Contradiction; $n$ is never installed at any reachable state, and $\mathrm{target}_n = \bot$ at $\gamma$ because $k$'s only possible present provider is $n$ itself (Def. 58(2) again), which is not Active.

*Cycles with $c \ge 2$.* Fix the history $\gamma^0 \to \cdots \to \gamma^T = \gamma$ and let $[t_0, T]$ be the maximal interval with all of $C$ present throughout ($t_0$ exists: all are present at $T$; presence changes only at O-Insert/O-Remove).

*Claim 1: no member is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$.* The step into $\gamma^{t_0}$ is an O-Insert (orchestrator's or a registration's) of some member $n_\ell$ — at $t_0 - 1$ some member was absent and at $t_0$ all are present, and only O-Insert adds presence — and its conclusion gives $\theta_{n_\ell} = \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ at $t_0$: $n_\ell$ is not installed. (If $t_0 = 0$ the registry is empty and the claim is vacuous, but $C \neq \varnothing$ forces $t_0 > 0$.) Now the *instantaneous propagation*: suppose some $n_i$ is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$. Then $k_i \in d_{n_i}$, and by Lemma 8 the resolver $m = \omega_{n_i}(k_i)$ is present, installed, with $k_i \in p_m$. Also $k_i \in p_{n_{i-1}}$ and $n_{i-1}$ is present (co-presence). By Def. 58(2), two present fibers with a common provision key are equal: $m = n_{i-1}$. So $n_{i-1}$ is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$. Iterating around the cycle ($c$ applications), *every* member is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$ — including $n_\ell$. Contradiction.

*Claim 2: "no member installed" is preserved on $[t_0, T]$.* Suppose it holds at $\gamma^{t}$ ($t_0 \le t < T$) and fails at $\gamma^{t+1}$. By Lemma 54(4) the step is an L-Begin of some $n_i$, whose premise $\mathrm{target}_{n_i}(\gamma^t) \neq \bot$ gives $\gamma^t \vDash d_{n_i}$, so $k_i$ has an Active — hence installed — provider $m$ at $\gamma^t$ with $k_i \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$; as in Claim 1, $m = n_{i-1}$ (both present on the interval), so $n_{i-1}$ is installed at $\gamma^t$ — contradicting the hypothesis at $t$.

Claims 1–2 give "no member installed at $\gamma$". Finally $\mathrm{target}_{n_i}(\gamma) = \bot$: $k_i$'s possible present providers at $\gamma$ are fibers $m$ with $k_i \in p_m$; $n_{i-1}$ is one, and any other present one would violate Def. 58(2) against $n_{i-1}$; so the only candidate is $n_{i-1}$, not Active (not even installed); hence $k_i \notin \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_\gamma)$, $\neg(\gamma \vDash d_{n_i})$, and Def. 46 gives $\bot$. $\square$

**Lemma 10 (frozen cone).** Let $\gamma$ be reachable and let $c = m_0 \prec m_1 \prec \cdots \prec m_j = n$ be a $\prec$-path with all $m_i \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$ and $m_0$ on a $\prec$-cycle within $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$. Then $n$ is not installed at $\gamma$ and $\mathrm{target}_n(\gamma) = \bot$; hence (as in Lemma 9) no lifecycle rule applies at $n$ and $n$'s quiet clause holds.

*Proof.* Induction on $j$; $j = 0$ is Lemma 9. Step: assume $m_{j-1}$ is not installed at $\gamma$ (IH — note the IH is *instantaneous at $\gamma$*, which suffices because every inference below reads $\gamma$ alone). Let $k \in p_{m_{j-1}} \cap d_n$ witness $m_{j-1} \prec n$. If $n$ were installed at $\gamma$, Lemma 8 would give a present installed resolver $m = \omega_n(k)$ with $k \in p_m$; both $m$ and $m_{j-1}$ present with $k \in p_m \cap p_{m_{j-1}}$ forces $m = m_{j-1}$ (Def. 58(2)) — installed, contradicting the IH. And $\mathrm{target}_n(\gamma) = \bot$ exactly as in Lemma 9's last paragraph, with $m_{j-1}$ in the role of $n_{i-1}$. $\square$

**The per-fiber and attribution bounds, restated and reproved.** Thm. 66's proof establishes two counting claims, (A) and (B), inside its own text; since we must use them outside the theorem's acyclicity hypothesis, we restate and reprove them here rather than cite proof-internal steps.

**Lemma 11 (per-fiber bound; Thm. 66's (A), acyclicity-free).** Let a lifecycle-only step sequence be given with $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$, and let $[a,b]$ be a maximal interval of it on which $\mathrm{target}^t_n$ is constant, $= \omega^*$. Then at most $K + 4$ steps in $[a,b]$ act on $n$.

*Proof.* Case on $\theta_n^a$, reading Table 1's $\theta$-columns; every transition of $n$ rewrites $\theta_n$ (fifth column), so the steps acting on $n$ in the interval trace a path in the state diagram (Fig. 2) whose edges are the rules, and it suffices to bound the longest path consistent with a constant target.

*From $\mathsf{Active}(-,\omega)$ with $\omega \neq \omega^*$:* the only rule at Active is L-Leave (its premise $\mathrm{target} \neq \omega$ holds); then at $\mathsf{Unloading}$ only L-Unload (L-Unload has no target premise); this lands in $\mathsf{Inactive}(\zeta)$. If $\omega^* = \bot$ or $\zeta = \xi$, no rule applies at $n$ any more within the interval (L-Begin needs $\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ and $\mathrm{target} \neq \bot$): $2$ steps. Otherwise one L-Begin (writing the committed view $\omega^*$ — the target itself, so no later rule sees disagreement), then at most $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$ landings (L-Iter at most $K-1$ times then L-Finish, since each landing consumes one iteration of $e_n$ and the continuation chain has length $\le K$; or an L-Raise at some landing), and — only on the L-Raise branch — one further L-Unload into $\mathsf{Inactive}(\xi)$, after which nothing applies. Within the interval no L-Divert or L-Leave can fire after the L-Begin: their premises need $\mathrm{target} \neq \omega$, but the committed view written was $\omega^*$ and the target is constant at $\omega^*$. Total $\le 2 + 1 + K + 1 = K + 4$.

*From $\mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega)$ with $\omega \neq \omega^*$:* an L-Divert (or L-Raise) replaces the L-Leave above; the remainder is a suffix of the same path: $\le K + 4$. *From $\mathsf{Active}(-,\omega^*)$, $\mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega^*)$, $\mathsf{Unloading}$, $\mathsf{Inactive}$:* each is a state of the path above, and the reachable continuation within the interval is a suffix of it: $\le K + 4$. (At $\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ with $\omega^* = \bot$, and at $\mathsf{Inactive}(\xi)$, zero steps.) $\square$

**Lemma 12 (turn attribution; Thm. 66's (B), acyclicity-free form).** In a lifecycle-only step sequence, if $\mathrm{target}^t_n \neq \mathrm{target}^{t+1}_n$ and step $t$ acts on $m$, then $m \prec n$ (possibly $m = n$ only when $n \prec n$) or step $t$ writes $\tau_n$; and the $\tau_n$ case occurs at most once in the whole sequence.

*Proof.* By Def. 46, $\mathrm{target}_n$ is a function of $\tau_n$ and of the tables of the Active fibers at the keys of $d_n$ (through $\gamma \vDash d_n$ and $\mathrm{provider}_k$, $k \in d_n$). If the value changes at step $t$, one of those inputs changed. $\tau_n$: Table 1's fifth column writes $\tau$ only at O-Retire — within a lifecycle-only sequence, only as the inverse a registration's accumulator or a $\mathsf{register}^{-1}$ item takes (Def. 47) — and $\tau$ is monotone, written only at $\top$ (Lemma 54(5)), so it turns at most once ever; that is the "one turn $\tau_n$ affords". Otherwise some $k \in d_n$ changed provision status: either $\sigma_m$ changed at a fiber $m$ with $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \cup$ (post-step) $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m)$ — by Lemma 54(1) tables change only at the acting fiber, so $m$ is the acting fiber, and $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$ gives $k \in p_m \cap d_n$, i.e. $m \prec n$ — or $m$'s Active-membership changed, i.e. $\theta_m$ was written, which happens only at steps acting on $m$ (fifth column), with the same $k \in p_m \cap d_n$ conclusion ($m$ was or becomes the Active provider of $k$, and providers satisfy $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$). A registration's O-Insert adds a fresh non-Active fiber and moves no target (its entry provides nothing yet, $\sigma = \varnothing$). $\square$

**Theorem 5 (generalized progress; strengthens [SZC26] Thm. 66).** Let $\gamma^\dagger$ be reachable, let every fiber satisfy $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$, and let the set $N$ of names ever present along any lifecycle-only continuation be finite (both hold for the test language: scripts and component payloads are finite data, so iteration counts and the registration tree are structurally bounded — the payload nesting of $\mathsf{register}$ steps is a finite tree with branching $\le$ script length). Then:

1. *(No deadlock.)* If $\neg\mathrm{quiet}(\gamma)$ at a state $\gamma$ reachable from $\gamma^\dagger$ by lifecycle steps, some lifecycle rule applies at $\gamma$.
2. *(Termination.)* Every maximal lifecycle-only step sequence from $\gamma^\dagger$ is finite.

Consequently every settle of $\mathbb S$ ends, after finitely many steps, in a quiescent state. **No $\prec$-acyclicity is assumed.** The calculus thus quiesces on cyclic configurations not by Thm. 66's bound but by unsatisfiability — the cycle freezes.

*Proof.* (1) Let $n$ violate its quiet clause (eq. 45). Reading Table 1 against the four kinds, exactly as in Thm. 66's no-deadlock case analysis (which nowhere uses acyclicity): if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n \neq \bot$, L-Begin applies; if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega_n)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n = \omega_n$, whichever of L-Iter, L-Finish, L-Raise the iterator's value selects applies; if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega_n)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n \neq \omega_n$, L-Raise (if the iteration raises) or L-Divert applies; if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Active}(-,\omega_n)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n \neq \omega_n$, L-Leave applies. If no fiber is of these kinds, some $m_0$ has $\theta_{m_0} = \mathsf{Unloading}(-,-,-)$. Construct $m_0, m_1, \dots$: given $m_j$ in Unloading, either $\neg\mathrm{relied}_{m_j}$ — L-Unload applies, stop — or there are $m_{j+1} \neq m_j$, $k_j \in d_{m_{j+1}}$ with $\mathrm{installed}_{m_{j+1}}$ and $\omega_{m_{j+1}}(k_j) = m_j$; by Lemma 8, $k_j \in p_{m_j}$, so $m_j \prec m_{j+1}$; and $m_{j+1}$, being installed and (by the standing case assumption) of none of the four kinds above, is itself in Unloading, so the construction continues. If the construction never stopped, finiteness of $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$ would force a repetition, exhibiting a $\prec$-cycle $\{m_i, \dots, m_{j}\}$ all of whose members are *installed and co-present at $\gamma$* — contradicting Lemma 9. (This replaces Thm. 66's appeal to acyclicity: the chain is $\prec$-increasing *and consists of installed fibers*, and installed fibers span no cycle.) So the construction stops, and a rule applies.

(2) Suppose some maximal lifecycle-only sequence from $\gamma^\dagger$ is infinite. $N$ is finite and every step acts on some name, so some fiber $n_{(0)}$ takes infinitely many steps. By Lemma 11, an interval of constant $\mathrm{target}_{n_{(0)}}$ contains $\le K+4$ of them, so $\mathrm{target}_{n_{(0)}}$ turns infinitely often. By Lemma 12 each turn beyond the single $\tau$-turn is a step acting on some $m \prec n_{(0)}$; the candidates are finitely many (names in $N$), so some $n_{(1)} \prec n_{(0)}$ takes infinitely many steps. Iterating produces $n_{(0)} \succ n_{(1)} \succ n_{(2)} \succ \cdots$, all with infinitely many steps; $N$ finite forces $n_{(i)} = n_{(j)}$ for some $i < j$, exhibiting a $\prec$-cycle $C$ every member of which takes infinitely many steps. Each member is present from its (unique within the sequence — no removals occur: O-Remove is an orchestration rule and registrations' inverses are O-Retires, Def. 47) insertion onward, so from some index $T^\flat$ on, all of $C$ are co-present. A member taking a step at $t \ge T^\flat$ is, at $\gamma^t$ or $\gamma^{t+1}$, installed (every lifecycle rule other than L-Begin has premise $\theta \neq \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$, and L-Begin's successor is installed) — at a reachable state where all of $C$ is co-present. That contradicts Lemma 9. Hence no infinite sequence exists; by (1) the endpoint of a maximal sequence is quiescent. $\square$

**Remark 3 (relation to Thm. 66's measure).** Within Thm. 66's hypotheses ($\prec$ acyclic), the quantitative bound stands as stated there: $S(n) \le (K+4)(V(n)+1)$ with $V(n) \le 1 + \sum_{m \prec n} S(m)$, closed by the well-founded recursion $B(n) = (K+4)(2 + \sum_{m \prec n} B(m))$, so $\sum_n S(n) \le \sum_n B(n)$ — an explicit rank. Theorem 5 trades the explicit rank for an infinite-descent argument in exchange for dropping acyclicity; where a rank is wanted in the general case, one is induced a posteriori: order the finitely many maximal sequences' lengths (all finite by the theorem) — but no *uniform* closed-form bound is claimed for cyclic $\prec$, and none is needed below.

**Theorem 6 (termination direction).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ and $\mathbb I \models T^\star$. Then for every script $\rho$ of the §3 language: every fair settle of $\mathbb I$ during $\rho$ terminates, and it returns at a state whose abstraction is quiescent with the matched report. In particular $\mathbb I$ diverges exactly where $\mathbb S$ does — nowhere on this language — and $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho)$ contains a $\mathsf{Diverge}$ outcome iff $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho)$ does (both never).

*Proof.* Fix a fair settle of $\mathbb I$ and suppose its run is infinite. By Lemma 7 it has infinitely many tagged steps; let $e_j$ denote its prefix up to the $j$-th tagged step. Each $e_j$ is a finite tagged execution, and Theorem 4 matches it by a specification execution $d_j$; moreover the matchings can be taken coherent (each $d_{j+1}$ extending $d_j$: the type-F argument of Theorem 4 extends the *given* match by one step — this is how the induction there runs — so a single chain $d_1 \sqsubseteq d_2 \sqsubseteq \cdots$ exists). During a settle, the tagged steps are lifecycle-tagged (the script offers no input until the settle returns; orchestration tags cannot occur; CF1's tagging admits no third kind), so $\bigcup_j d_j$ is an infinite lifecycle-only specification sequence from the matched settle-start state — a reachable state of the test language, where $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$ and $N$ finite hold structurally (Theorem 5's preamble). That contradicts Theorem 5(2). So the run is finite; being maximal for the settle, its last state $u$ has no enabled offer left un-taken under fairness, and by Theorem 4 (type B) every specification-enabled rule at the matched $\gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}A(u)$ is offered at $u$ — so none is enabled at $\gamma$, i.e. $\gamma$ is quiescent by Theorem 5(1)'s contrapositive; CF6(i) returns the settle there, and the report matches by Theorem 4's report clause. For the last claim: $\mathbb S$ yields no $\mathsf{Diverge}$ on any $\rho$ by Theorem 5, and $\mathbb I$ yields none by the above. $\square$

Termination-sensitivity is not decorative: necessity survives its deletion, sufficiency does not. The exact statement:

**Proposition 2 (what termination-sensitivity adds).** Let $\mathbf{conf}^-$ be conformance with the termination clauses deleted (Definition 2's per-script clause (ii) and Definition 3's clauses 4 (second half) and 5), and let $\models^-$ be Definition 2's passing judgment with its divergence requirement deleted. Then: (i) Theorem 4 and Theorem 2's necessity direction survive the deletion verbatim — finite-prefix matching never used the termination clauses. (ii) Sufficiency does **not**: there is a system $\mathbb J \notin \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ but satisfying CF1–CF5 and CF7 with $f^{\mathbb J} = f^{\mathbb S}$, whose settle never returns (it interleaves the matched tagged steps with an infinite untagged stutter after quiescence, violating only CF6(i)); $\mathbb J \models^- T^\star$ and $\mathbb J$'s finite behaviors are exactly $\mathbb S$'s, yet $\mathbb J$ produces no quiescent report at all and $\mathbb J \not\mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$. Dually, a system violating only fairness can starve one of two enabled rules forever, never realizing an offered outcome, and passes every per-run check while its $\mathrm{Out}$-set is a strict subset of $\mathbb S$'s. Hence the termination-and-fairness content of the bisimulation is exactly: (a) CF6 as an audit obligation, and (b) the passing judgment's termination clause as the executable judgment — clause (b) is checkable by the suite (case (e) of Lemma 5), clause (a) is not, and the two together are what upgrade finite-prefix bisimulation to the termination-sensitive $\mathbf{conf}$.

*Proof of (ii).* $\mathbb J$ as described satisfies the finite-prefix clauses by construction (its tagged steps are $\mathbb S$'s); it fails the termination clause of $\mathbf{conf}$ on any script whose settle $\mathbb S$ completes (every script, by Theorem 5), since $\mathbb J$ has a divergent outcome and no $\mathsf{Done}$ outcome there; and it fails no $\models^-$ verdict, as those never await the settle's return. $\square$

### 6.6 Assembly

*Proof of Theorem 2.*

**Necessity: $\mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S \implies \mathbb I \models T^\star$.** Fix $(\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s}) \in T^\star$. By Definition 2, $\mathbf{conf}$ supplies an abstraction $A$ under which $\mathbb I$ and $\mathbb S$ are observation-bisimilar (Definition 3), entailing for *every* finite script — in particular for $\rho_{r,s}$ — its clauses. We derive the two conjuncts of passing (Definition 2).

*(1) Every fair choice is visited and classified.* The judgment $\models$ quantifies over all fair schedulers; discharging that quantifier exhaustively is a property of the test *harness* executing the judgment (§7's exhaustive-schedule driver), not a property of $\mathbb I$, and nothing here needs proving about $\mathbb I$.

*(2) $V_{r,s}(\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s}))$ holds.* $V_{r,s}$ is, by construction (§5.4), the predicate "$\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s}) \equiv \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho_{r,s})$, and every $\mathbb I$-run terminates where every $\mathbb S$-run does". We prove outcome-set equality; the termination conjunct is then Definition 3's clause 5 directly.

$\subseteq$: Let $o \in \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s})$, produced by a run $\pi$ under some fair scheduler. **Why the quantification over $\mathbb I$'s runs is covered:** $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}$ quantifies over *all* fair schedulers, whereas the bisimulation clauses of Definition 3 are stated per transition; the bridge is that a bisimulation matches *every* transition $\mathbb I$ actually takes, whichever scheduler chose it — the forward clauses quantify over all $\mathbb I$-transitions from every related pair, not over a preferred run. So induct along $\pi$: at each orchestration input, clause 1 extends the matched $\mathbb S$-run and matches the refusal log; at each lifecycle transition tagged $(r', n')$, clause 2 matches it by the $r'$-transition of $\mathbb S$ at the related state with equivalent events and successor abstraction — and equivalent *certificates*, since a certificate is a function of the tag, the normalized window, and the normalized update, all matched (the window by the successor-abstraction relation and Lemma 1; the update by the matched abstract successor; no CF4 is needed here beyond $\mathbb I$'s certificates being part of its observed word, which $\equiv$ compares); untagged steps contribute nothing (clause 6). If $\pi$ ends in a completed settle, clause 4 matches $q$; if in a refusal, the refusal half of clause 1; divergence cannot occur (clause 5 with Theorem 5 on the $\mathbb S$ side — and if it did occur it would be matched by clause 5 anyway). The assembled $\mathbb S$-run yields $o' \equiv o$.

$\supseteq$: Let $o' \in \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho_{r,s})$. By the converse clause 3, induct along $o'$'s run to build an offered run of $\mathbb I$ with matching labels, events, certificates, and report. It remains to note the offered run is realized in $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}$: $\mathrm{Out}$ is the outcome set over all fair schedulers admitted by $\mathbb I$ (Definition 1), and a finite offered run is realized by some admitted scheduler that selects exactly its choices and continues fairly afterwards; its settle completes by clause 5. Hence $o' \in \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s})$ up to $\equiv$.

Both inclusions give set equality; $V_{r,s}$ holds. As $(r,s)$ was arbitrary, $\mathbb I \models T^\star$.

**Sufficiency: $\mathbb I \models T^\star \implies \mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$.** Define, with $A$ the CF1 abstraction,

$$R_A := \{(u, \gamma) \mid u \in \mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I),\ \gamma \in \Gamma_{\mathbb S},\ \gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}\cdot A(u) \text{ for the renaming } \chi \text{ accumulated by the matched history of } u\}.$$

We verify the clauses of Definition 3.

1. *Initial states:* CF1's initial-state clause; the empty renaming.
2. *Each orchestration transition of $\mathbb I$ is matched by the corresponding transition or refusal of $\mathbb S$:* Theorem 4, absence of type-F deviations, orchestration case (each accepted input is matched with equal certificate and successor relation; each refusal is matched with equal reason — the table equality covers both polarities).
3. *Each lifecycle transition tagged $(r,n)$ is matched by the $r$-transition of $\mathbb S$ at $n$, with equivalent events, certificates, and successor abstraction:* Theorem 4, type-F lifecycle case.
4. *Conversely every enabled $\mathbb S$-transition has a correspondingly labeled implementation transition or refusal with $A$-related successor:* Theorem 4, absence of type-B deviations.
5. *Quiescent states have equivalent reports; settle returns exactly at abstract quiescence:* Theorem 4's report clause together with CF6(i)/(ii).
6. *Fair settling terminates or diverges on the same scripts:* Theorem 6 (both systems terminate on every script of the language). *Untagged steps:* CF1's tagging totality.

Clauses 2–4 hold at every $R_A$-related pair, not only along one run, because Theorem 4's induction is over *all* matched prefixes and Lemma 55 transports the matching across $\simeq$-related specification states (the calculus's own invariance lemma is natively an up-to technique). So $R_A$ is an observation bisimulation for the abstraction $A$; by Definition 2, $\mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$ (and the necessity argument above re-derives the entailed per-script conditions, in particular outcome equivalence and no-hidden-acceptance).

Necessity and sufficiency together give the biconditional, and Theorem 3 discharges the theorem's applicability to $\mathbb S$ itself (the tables $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ used throughout are those of a member of the class, so "table equality" is a statement between two members of one formalism, not a pun). $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 2 (the composite scenarios are corollaries, not axioms).** Every observation-level consequence of the [SZC26] metatheory on its stated domains — recovery exactness (Thm. 61/Cor. 62), ordering (Thm. 63), coherence (Thm. 64), progress (Thm. 66, and Theorem 5 beyond it), confluence (Thm. 73) — holds of every $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ with $\mathbb I \models T^\star$: by Theorem 2, $\mathbb I$'s observations equal $\mathbb S$'s, and the metatheorems constrain $\mathbb S$'s. In particular the composite reconfiguration-coherence chain (Lemmas 68/70/71/72 + Thm. 73(2) + Lemma 56) transfers verbatim: its statement is an observation predicate over scripts, and both sides of Theorem 2 are closed under it. $\square$
