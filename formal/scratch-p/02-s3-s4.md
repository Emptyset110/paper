## 3. The Plugin-System Interface

**Definition 1 (events and observations).** Fix countable disjoint sorts: names $\mathfrak N$, keys $K$, values $\mathbb V$, effect tags $\mathbb T$. The event alphabet is

$$\mathcal E ::= \mathsf{app}(n,t) \mid \mathsf{inv}(n,t) \mid \mathsf{rd}(n,k,v) \mid \mathsf{rderr}(n,k,\epsilon) \mid \mathsf{act}(n) \mid \mathsf{deact}(n) \mid \mathsf{val}(n,k)$$

with $\epsilon \in \{\mathsf{IA}, \mathsf{UD}\}$ (inactive resp. undeclared access). In addition, transitions emit *certificates* $\mathsf{cert}(r, \widehat s, \widehat a)$: the rule tag, the normalized signature of the window it fired at, and the normalized update abstract. An *outcome* of a run is $\mathsf{Done}(w,q)$, $\mathsf{Refused}(w,\lambda,q)$, or $\mathsf{Diverge}(u,v)$, where $w$ is the word of events and certificates, $q$ the quiescent report (statuses, outcomes, the store $\mathrm{st}(k) = \sigma_\gamma(k)$, refusal log), and $\lambda$ the refused input. $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb X}(\rho)$ is the outcome set of system $\mathbb X$ on orchestration script $\rho$ over all fair schedulers; $\equiv$ compares outcome sets up to sort-preserving renaming and the calculus's value equivalence, preserving ordinary event order exactly and comparing certificates after normalization.

*Convention (certificates are letters).* The word $w$ of an outcome contains both ordinary events and certificates, and $\equiv$ additionally preserves the relative order of certificates against ordinary events at the same fiber: a certificate is emitted synchronously at its transition, between the events of the preceding and following transitions. This evident reading is load-bearing in the reflection proof (§6.3).

**Definition 2 (plugin system; conformance; passing).** A *plugin system* is an LTS over orchestration labels $\{\mathsf{insert}, \mathsf{retire}, \mathsf{update}, \mathsf{setval}, \mathsf{isolate}, \mathsf{intercept}\}$ and internal lifecycle steps, with the instrumentation of Definition 1. The specification $\mathbb S$ is the calculus so instrumented, with components given as *scripts*: finite step lists over $\{\mathsf{provide}, \mathsf{track}, \mathsf{read}, \mathsf{setval}, \mathsf{register}, \mathsf{raise}, \mathsf{raiseUnless}\}$, one step per iteration of Def. 51 (a tracked inverse may itself read — the read-on-revert form — which §5 shows the suite requires). $\mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$ iff there is an *observation bisimulation* for $\mathbb I$'s abstraction (Definition 3 below). Its per-script content, entailed by the bisimulation (the entailment is proved as the necessity direction, §6.6), is: for every script $\rho$, (i) $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho) \equiv \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho)$ restricted to $\mathsf{Done}/\mathsf{Refused}$ outcomes, and (ii) $\mathbb I$ has a $\mathsf{Diverge}$ outcome on $\rho$ only where $\mathbb S$ does. A test is $t = (\rho_t, V_t)$ with $V_t$ decidable on outcomes; $\mathbb I \models t$ iff **no run of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho_t$ diverges at a settle point** and every outcome satisfies $V_t$.

**Definition 3 (observation bisimulation).** Let $\mathbb I$ carry a total abstraction $A : \mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I) \to \Gamma_{\mathbb S}$ and rule tags on its transitions (CF1 of Definition 4 supplies both for the implementations we quantify over; for $\mathbb S$, $A = \mathrm{id}$). An *observation bisimulation* is a relation $R \subseteq \mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I) \times \Gamma_{\mathbb S}$ such that the initial states are related via $A(i_0) = \gamma^0$, and at every related pair:

1. each accepted orchestration input of $\mathbb I$ is matched by the same input's $\mathbb S$-transition with $\equiv$-equal emission, equal normalized certificate, and related successors; each refusal of $\mathbb I$ is matched by an $\mathbb S$-refusal with the same reason;
2. each lifecycle transition of $\mathbb I$ tagged $(r,n)$ is matched by the $r$-transition of $\mathbb S$ at the corresponding fiber, with $\equiv$-equal events, equal normalized certificate, and related successors;
3. conversely, every $\mathbb S$-enabled orchestration or lifecycle transition has a correspondingly labeled transition or refusal *offered* by $\mathbb I$, with related successor — an implementation of the calculus must offer every enabled calculus transition, even if its production scheduler deterministically selects one of them in a particular execution;
4. related quiescent states have $\equiv$-equal reports, and $\mathbb I$'s settle returns exactly at states whose abstraction is quiescent;
5. fair settling terminates or diverges on the same scripts;
6. untagged internal steps of $\mathbb I$ change no abstract field, emit nothing, and are matched by no $\mathbb S$-step.

Clauses 1–2 name certificates explicitly (they are letters of $w$, so "equivalent events" silently includes them; making the clause explicit removes an ambiguity the assembly would otherwise have to argue around). The divergence clause in $\models$ (Definition 2) closes a vacuity: without it, an implementation that never quiesces passes every suite. Conformance is a preorder; the bisimulation is what the sufficiency proof establishes clause by clause (§6.6) and what necessity consumes.

## 4. Impossibility, and the Clause-Factored Class

**Theorem 1 (no finite suite characterizes unrestricted conformance).** No finite suite $T$ characterizes conformance over unrestricted implementations: for every finite suite $T$ that the specification itself passes ($\mathbb S \models T$; a suite $\mathbb S$ fails characterizes nothing, as $\mathbb I = \mathbb S$ already witnesses), there is an implementation $\mathbb I_T$ with $\mathbb I_T \models T$ and $\mathbb I_T \not\mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$.

*Proof.* Fix $T = \{(\rho_1, V_1), \dots, (\rho_j, V_j)\}$ finite with $\mathbb S \models T$. Scripts are finite data, so the components occurring in $\rho_1, \dots, \rho_j$ (including every component payload of a $\mathsf{register}$ step, which is itself part of the script's finite data) are finitely many; let

$$N := \max \{\, |d| \;:\; (d,p,S) \text{ a component occurring in some } \rho_i \,\} \quad (N := 0 \text{ if none occurs}).$$

Define $\mathbb I_T$ as the system with the same states, instrumentation, and transition relation as $\mathbb S$, except that L-Begin at a fiber $n$ carries the additional premise $|d_n| \le N$: for fibers declaring more than $N$ dependency keys, L-Begin never fires (and no certificate for it is emitted, exactly as for any disabled rule).

*Claim 1: $\mathbb I_T \models T$.* Fix $i$ and any fair scheduler. Every fiber ever present in a run of $\rho_i$ instantiates a component of $\rho_i$ (every fiber enters by O-Insert, whether the orchestrator's or a registration's, and both draw their component data from the script — Def. 53's discipline), so every fiber $n$ in every reachable state of the run has $|d_n| \le N$ and the added premise is satisfied at every L-Begin window. By induction on the length of the run, the transition relations of $\mathbb I_T$ and $\mathbb S$ coincide on all states reachable during $\rho_i$, step for step, with identical emissions, certificates, refusals, reports, and settling behavior. Hence $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I_T}(\rho_i) = \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho_i)$, and $\mathbb I_T$ diverges at a settle point of $\rho_i$ exactly where $\mathbb S$ does — nowhere, since $\mathbb S \models T$. So $\mathbb I_T$ passes every $(\rho_i, V_i)$.

*Claim 2: $\mathbb I_T \not\mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$.* Let $k_0, \dots, k_N$ be $N{+}1$ distinct keys and consider the script $\rho^\dagger$ that inserts, for each $j \le N$, a provider $P_j := (\varnothing, \{k_j\}, [\mathsf{provide}(k_j, v)])$, settles, then inserts the consumer $C := (\{k_0, \dots, k_N\}, \varnothing, [])$ and settles. In $\mathbb S$, after the first settles every $k_j$ is Active-provided, so $\mathrm{target}_C \neq \bot$ and the final settle runs L-Begin and L-Finish at $C$: every $\mathbb S$-outcome's word contains $\mathsf{cert}(\text{L-Begin}, \cdot, \cdot)$ at $C$ followed by $\mathsf{act}(C)$, and the report shows $C$ Active. In $\mathbb I_T$, $|d_C| = N{+}1 > N$, so L-Begin never fires at $C$: no run's word contains $\mathsf{act}(C)$, and every report shows $C$ pending. (Both systems quiesce: with L-Begin disabled at $C$, $C$'s quiet clause cannot hold — $\mathrm{target}_C \neq \bot$ — so, strictly, $\mathbb I_T$'s settle is read as returning when no *$\mathbb I_T$-rule* applies, which is its CF6-analogue; under either reading of its settle, no outcome of $\mathbb I_T$ on $\rho^\dagger$ is $\equiv$-equal to any outcome of $\mathbb S$, since $\equiv$ preserves the letter $\mathsf{act}(C)$ and the report's status field.) By Definition 2's per-script content (i) — entailed by any bisimulation — $\mathbb I_T \not\mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$.

The same construction works with any other finite datum of the suite in place of the dependency bound: an implementation that treats key spellings outside the finite set occurring in $T$ as unsatisfiable, or that disables a rule once the registry exceeds the largest size $T$ ever produces, passes $T$ and deviates on a fresh script by the identical two-claim argument. No finite suite escapes the schema, because every finite suite determines finite bounds on the data its scripts mention. $\blacksquare$

*Integration note.* The full proofs deliverable (`proofs.md`) does not contain a proof of this theorem; the body proof above is elaborated from the manuscript's own proof sketch. The elaboration forced one proviso to become explicit that the sketch elided: the suite must be one the specification passes (unsound suites characterize nothing, and for them the theorem's witness need not exist — every implementation may fail $T$).

The theorem locates exactly what a finite characterization must assume: that the implementation cannot *count*, *spell*, or otherwise read its state except through the rule's own atoms — $\mathbb I_T$ reads the cardinality $|d_n|$, which no premise of Table 1 reads (§6.1). We make this a definition.

**Definition 4 (clause-factored implementations).** $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ iff:

- **CF1 (abstraction & tagging).** There is a total abstraction $A : \mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I) \to \Gamma_{\mathbb S}$ with $A(\gamma^0_{\mathbb I}) = \gamma^0$, preserving every report-visible field; every implementation transition is tagged with the rule it implements, untagged transitions change no abstract field and emit nothing, and certificates are emitted per Definition 1.
- **CF2 (equivariance).** Behavior commutes with sort-preserving bijections of names, keys, values, and tags fixing the reserved constants.
- **CF3 (signature factorization).** For each rule $r$ there is a table $f_r^{\mathbb I} : \Sigma_r \to \mathrm{Decision}_r$ such that tagged applicability, updates, and emissions at any window are the *lift* of $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ over the window's occurrence multiset $\kappa_r(W)$ (Definitions 5–8), combined by the row's declared folds (pointwise products over keys; existential folds over witnesses; one fixed binary composition for the accumulator), with order data drawn from the window's frame. In particular the table cannot inspect registry cardinality, allocation order, unnormalized spelling, or the number of irrelevant witnesses.
- **CF4 (instrumentation faithfulness).** Certificates truthfully and synchronously report the actual $(r, \widehat s, \widehat a)$ of each tagged transition — the occurrence consulted and the update performed — and the certificate abstract $\widehat a$ ranges over all of $\widehat U_r$, covering the update aspects that events and reports do not determine (*certificate adequacy*; §6.3 proves this clause is not optional, and names the adapter obligation it imposes).
- **CF5 (canonical reachability).** Renaming-invariant behavior on the canonical experiments of §5.
- **CF6 (fair, exact settling).** The scheduler is fair and settle terminates exactly on quiescent configurations (the exact reading is fixed in §6.5).
- **CF7 (witness fidelity / refusal discipline).** Orchestration inputs are refused exactly where the rule's premises fail, with the refusal observable; the inverses supplied by effects implement the witnessed inverse relation of Def. 51/eq. 49 (each inverse defined where its effect ran).

Each obligation is auditable against code: CF3 rules out branching on $|d_n|$, on registry cardinality, or on spellings (Theorem 1's implementation violates it); CF2 rules out reserved names; CF6 pins the scheduler. The class is far from vacuous: it contains $\mathbb S$ itself (Theorem 3) and all twenty-seven deviant calculi of §8 (Appendix F), which is what makes necessity meaningful.
