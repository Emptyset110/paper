## 0. Preliminaries

### 0.1 Objects and conventions

Throughout, $\gamma,\delta$ range over registry states (Def. 45/49 of [SZC26]), $F_\gamma$ is the registry, $\sigma_\gamma = \bigcup\{\sigma_m \mid \theta_m = \mathsf{Active}(-,-)\}$ (eq. 40), $\mathrm{target}_n$ is Def. 46 read on the four-state space of Def. 49, $\mathrm{installed}_n(\gamma) :\Leftrightarrow \theta_n \neq \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$ and $\mathrm{failed}_n(\gamma) :\Leftrightarrow \exists\xi.\ \theta_n = \mathsf{Inactive}(\xi)$ (eq. 44), $\mathrm{relied}_n$ is Def. 50, $\mathrm{quiet}$ is eq. 45. Steps factor as $\gamma^{t+1} = \mathrm{edit}^t(\Psi^t(\gamma^t))$ (eq. 52), with $\Psi^t$ and the edited control fields given rule-by-rule by Table 1 (p. 40). The ten rules are

$$\mathrm{Rules} = \{\text{O-Insert}, \text{O-Retire}, \text{O-Remove}, \text{L-Begin}, \text{L-Iter}, \text{L-Finish}, \text{L-Divert}, \text{L-Raise}, \text{L-Leave}, \text{L-Unload}\}.$$

$\simeq$ is Def. 33 read on states as eq. 53; $\approx$ is the control-field-forgetting relation of p. 39. A *sort-preserving bijection* is a tuple $\chi = (\chi_{\mathfrak N}, \chi_K, \chi_{\mathbb V}, \chi_{\mathbb T})$ of bijections of names, keys, values, and tags fixing the reserved constants ($\mathsf{root}$; the outcome constants $\bot$ and the error constructors of $\Xi$; the realm constants of Def. 28 that name no key). $\chi\cdot\gamma$ is the state with every occurrence of a name/key/value/tag replaced by its image (registry reindexed by $\chi_{\mathfrak N}$, tables by $\chi_K$, etc.), and $\chi$ acts on events, certificates, scripts, and reports letterwise.

The observation vocabulary is PAPER_REWRITE §2.3–§2.4: ordinary events $\mathcal E$, refusal events, the certificates $\mathsf{cert}(r,\widehat s,\widehat a)$, quiescent reports, and the three outcome constructors $\mathsf{Done}(w,q)$, $\mathsf{Refused}(w,\lambda,q)$, $\mathsf{Diverge}(u,v)$; $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb X}(\rho)$ is the outcome set over all fair schedulers; $\equiv$ is PAPER_REWRITE §3.1. **Convention (certificates are letters).** Per PAPER_REWRITE §2.4, the word $w$ of an outcome contains both ordinary events and certificates, and $\equiv$ compares certificates after normalization and preserves ordinary event order exactly; we additionally record the (evident, but load-bearing) reading that $\equiv$ preserves the *relative order of certificates against ordinary events at the same fiber* — a certificate is emitted synchronously at its transition (CF4), between the events of the preceding and following transitions.

### 0.2 Windows, occurrences, decisions, and lift

This subsection fixes, once, the formal shape that §1 instantiates per rule. It refines PAPER_REWRITE Definition 3 only by making explicit what that definition leaves implicit (the guard folds, the update codomain, and the location of order information); no content is changed. Deviations from MASTER's drafts are logged in §7.

**Definition D2.0.1 (window).** For $r \in \mathrm{Rules}$ and a state $\gamma$ with acting fiber $n$ (for O-Insert: acting data $(n,\pi,(d,p,e))$), the *window* $W_r(\gamma,n)$ is the tuple of exactly the abstract fields the Table-1 row's premises read and its writes touch, together with the row's *combination frame*: the index sets of its finite-map operations (the key set $d_n$ for the target product; the domain of the existential folds), and the *order data* of its order-sensitive operators (the iterator position; the accumulator's composition order as a finite list). §1 inventories $W_r$ per rule against the row.

**Definition D2.0.2 (occurrences; $\kappa_r$).** For each $r$, D1 supplies a finite alphabet $\Sigma_r$ of *occurrences*; by D1's Σ-table for rule $r$ we use exactly these properties: **(D1-A)** $\Sigma_r$ is finite and its occurrence classes cover exactly the guard atoms and write inputs of Table 1's row for $r$, in the granularity of MASTER's signature drafts (each occurrence records: the truth values of the row's primitive scalar guard atoms; the step constructor selected, if any; the equality pattern among the finitely many names, keys, values, and tags read by that atomic decision; witness/counterexample status of each existential or universal map condition; and the normalized abstract update expected on one representative key, edge, or accumulator item). The map

$$\kappa_r : W_r \to \mathcal M_{\mathrm{fin}}(\Sigma_r)$$

sends a window to the multiset containing: one *scalar occurrence* (the row's scalar-atom valuation, step constructor, and scalar equality pattern); one *per-key occurrence* for each key in each pointwise index set of the frame; one *witness occurrence* for each element of each existential domain that satisfies the witness predicate (conflict key, child edge, reliance edge); and one *per-item occurrence* for each accumulator item. $\kappa_r$ forgets the frame's order data and the concrete identities (it keeps only equality patterns); the frame keeps them. §1 spells $\kappa_r$ out per rule.

**Definition D2.0.3 (decisions).** For each $r$, the per-occurrence decision codomain is

$$\mathrm{Decision}_r := \{\mathsf{block}(\varrho)\}_{\varrho} \;\uplus\; \{\mathsf{pass}\} \times \widehat U_r \times \widehat E_r^{\,*},$$

where $\varrho$ ranges over the finitely many refusal reasons of the row (one per premise schema), $\widehat U_r$ is the finite set of normalized per-occurrence update constructors of the row (enumerated per rule in §1: e.g. for L-Begin, $\mathsf{commit}(k\text{-pattern} \mapsto \text{provider-pattern})$ and the state write $\theta_n \mapsto \mathsf{Reloading}(e_n,\mathrm{id}_\Gamma,\cdot)$; for L-Unload, $\mathsf{apply\mbox{-}inverse}(c)$ for each inverse class $c$ and the state write $\theta_n \mapsto \mathsf{Inactive}(\zeta)$), and $\widehat E_r$ the finite set of per-occurrence event constructors (with payload given by the occurrence's equality pattern). A *table* is a function $f_r : \Sigma_r \to \mathrm{Decision}_r$. $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ is the specification table read off Table 1's row (constructed rule-by-rule in §1); $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ is the implementation table postulated by CF3.

**Definition D2.0.4 (lift).** $\mathrm{lift}_r$ takes the window $W$ (with its frame) and a $\mathrm{Decision}_r$-valued family indexed by $\kappa_r(W)$'s occurrences, and produces the rule-level result:

1. *(Enablement.)* The row's guard formula, a fixed monotone Boolean combination: the conjunction of the scalar occurrence's required atoms, the conjunction over all per-key occurrences of "this occurrence's decision is $\mathsf{pass}$", and, for each existential schema, presence (for positive premises) or absence (for negative premises, i.e. O-Insert's disjointness, O-Remove's childlessness, L-Unload's $\neg\mathrm{relied}$) of a witness occurrence. If the formula is false the rule-level result is the refusal $\mathsf{block}(\varrho)$ of the leftmost failing premise (orchestration rules) resp. non-applicability (lifecycle rules).
2. *(Update.)* The row's $\mathrm{edit} \circ \Psi$ assembled from the per-occurrence updates: field assignments from the scalar occurrence's update; the finite-map product $\bigsqcup_k$ of per-key updates over the frame's index set (well-defined and order-irrelevant because per-key supports are disjoint singletons); the removal/extension of a registry entry; and, for the order-sensitive operators, the composition of per-item updates *in the frame's order data* (the iterator's $g \circ h$; the accumulator's LIFO application).
3. *(Emission.)* The concatenation of the per-occurrence emissions in the frame's order (a single landing's events; the accumulator's $\mathsf{inv}$ events in composition order followed by $\mathsf{deact}$).

These are exactly the finite-map unions, restrictions, existential folds, and accumulator composition operators of [SZC26] (PAPER_REWRITE Definition 3's closing paragraph); no other operator appears in §1.

**Remark D2.0.5 (where the order lives — a sharpening of the skeleton).** $\kappa_r(W)$ is a multiset and carries no order; the accumulator's composition order and the iterator's position are frame data of $W$, consumed by $\mathrm{lift}_r$. Consequently CF3 constrains the implementation's *per-occurrence* behavior through $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ and its *combination* behavior through the requirement that it use the specified $\mathrm{lift}_r$ — two separate obligations. The Transport Lemma (§2) is stated with exactly this split; a version claiming that $\kappa$-equal windows receive identical *whole-rule* results would be false for windows that agree as multisets but differ in accumulator order. See §7, correction (C3).

**Guards factor through the support.** For later reuse we record: clause 1 of Definition D2.0.4 depends on the family only through *which occurrence classes are present*, i.e. through $\mathrm{supp}(\kappa_r(W))$ and the per-class decisions — conjunctions and presence/absence tests are insensitive to multiplicities. This is the signature-level face of "no rule reads a cardinality" and is used in §1's per-row checks and in §2.

### 0.3 Normalization invariance of $\kappa_r$

**Lemma D2.0.6 ($\kappa$ is invariant under renaming and $\simeq/\approx$).** Let $r \in \mathrm{Rules}$, let $\chi$ be a sort-preserving bijection, and let $\gamma, \gamma'$ be states with $\gamma' \simeq \chi\cdot\gamma$ (eq. 53's reading of $\simeq$). Then for every $n$, $W_r(\gamma', \chi_{\mathfrak N}(n))$ and $W_r(\gamma,n)$ have equal images under $\kappa_r$:

$$\kappa_r\big(W_r(\gamma',\chi_{\mathfrak N}(n))\big) = \kappa_r\big(W_r(\gamma,n)\big),$$

and the two frames correspond under $\chi$ (same index-set images, same order data up to $\chi$).

*Proof.* We verify the claim for each of the five kinds of data an occurrence records (Definition D2.0.2 / PAPER_REWRITE Definition 3, items 1–5), quantifying over all occurrences of the window.

(1) *Scalar guard atoms.* By the premise inventory in the proof of Lemma 55 of [SZC26], every premise of every rule is of one of four kinds: a pattern match on $\theta_n$ or $\tau_n$; the component conditions of O-Insert; a condition mentioning $\mathrm{target}_n$ or $\mathrm{relied}_n$ (reading $\tau$, committed views inside the $\theta$'s, and $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m)$'s); or a read of $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$. A pattern match on a constructor is preserved by $\chi$ (constructors are reserved) and by $\simeq$ (eq. 53 keeps every control field up to $\simeq$ on its type, and $\theta$'s constructor is compared by equality). Membership and equality conditions on names/keys are preserved by bijections and read identically at $\simeq$-related states because $\simeq$ (eq. 53) demands agreement of $\mathrm{dom}(F)$ and of every control field. $\mathrm{target}$ and $\mathrm{relied}$ are functions of exactly those fields plus $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_\gamma)$, and Definition 33 relates coeffect contexts only where their domains agree; so each scalar atom has the same truth value at the two windows. Hence the scalar occurrences are equal (their recorded valuations coincide, and their recorded equality patterns coincide because a bijection preserves every equality and every inequality among the finitely many read identities — this is the defining property of an *equality pattern*).

(2) *Step constructors.* The iterator constituent of $\theta_n$ is compared by $\simeq$ as Definition 51 compares iterators: componentwise, $\mathsf{Nothing}$ with $\mathsf{Nothing}$, $\mathsf{Just}$ with $\mathsf{Just}$, and for the test-component language of §2.2 the step at the head is a data constructor preserved on the nose by $\chi$ (payloads renamed sortwise). So the recorded constructor is equal.

(3) *Equality patterns.* As in (1): bijections preserve equality patterns by definition; $\simeq$-variation of values is quotiented by the normalization $\kappa$ performs (occurrences record value *classes*, and Definition 33/24 makes $\simeq_k$-related values fall in the same class — this is what "normalized" means in Definition 3).

(4) *Witness status.* Each existential domain (conflict keys, child edges, reliance edges, satisfaction providers) is carried bijectively by $\chi$ onto the corresponding domain at $\chi\cdot\gamma$ (the domains are defined by equalities and memberships among carried fields), and $\simeq$ preserves each witness predicate by (1). Hence witness occurrences correspond one-to-one — equality *as multisets*, since the correspondence is a bijection of index sets.

(5) *Normalized per-item updates.* The expected update on a representative item is a constructor of $\widehat U_r$ with payload an equality pattern; by (1)–(4) these agree.

Finally, the frames: the index sets are carried bijectively by $\chi_K$/$\chi_{\mathfrak N}$, and the order data (iterator position, accumulator list order) are structural constituents of $\theta_n$ preserved by $\simeq$'s componentwise iterator comparison and untouched by renaming. $\square$

**Corollary D2.0.7 (reachable signatures are $\simeq/\chi$-stable).** If $\gamma$ is reachable in $\mathbb S$ and $s \in \kappa_r(W_r(\gamma,n))$, and $\gamma' \simeq \chi\cdot\gamma$ is also reachable, then the same $s$ lies in $\kappa_r(W_r(\gamma',\chi n))$. In particular the predicate "$s$ is exhibited at some reachable state" is well-defined on the quotient the calculus descends to (Lemma 55), and D1's reachability analysis **(D1-B)** — $s \in \Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$ iff some finite $\mathbb S$-derivation from the empty registry reaches a pointed window whose $\kappa_r$-image contains $s$ — is invariant under the renamings of Lemma 56. $\square$

### 0.4 Sort-extended equivariance of $\mathbb S$

Lemma 56 of [SZC26] is equivariance under name bijections $\chi : \mathfrak N \to \mathfrak N$ alone. CF2 demands equivariance under all four sorts. We prove the extension once; §1 cites it per rule.

**Lemma D2.0.8 (equivariance in all four sorts).** Let $\chi$ be a sort-preserving bijection. Then $\chi\cdot\gamma$ is a state, well formed where $\gamma$ is, and $\mathrm{step}^t = r(n)$ carries $\gamma^t$ to $\gamma^{t+1}$ iff $r(\chi_{\mathfrak N}(n))$ carries $\chi\cdot\gamma^t$ to $\chi\cdot\gamma^{t+1}$, with the emitted events and certificate carried to their $\chi$-images.

*Proof.* The name part is Lemma 56 verbatim. For keys, values, and tags we run Lemma 56's proof scheme over the remaining reads and writes, using the same premise inventory as in Lemma D2.0.6(1):

*Keys.* A premise reads a key only through: membership $k \in d_n$, $k \in p$, $k \in p_m$, $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m)$ (inside $\gamma \vDash d_n$ and $\mathrm{provider}_k$); emptiness of the intersection $p \cap p_m$ (O-Insert); equality of two committed/target views on their common domain $d_n$ (the $\mathrm{target}_n(\gamma) = \omega$ / $\neq \omega$ premises), which unfolds to per-key equalities of *names*; and the key parameter of an operation step (Defs. 23/29/31: $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma)$, $\rho(k) \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma)$, $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\rho)$). Each is a membership or equality, preserved by the bijection $\chi_K$ (with $\rho$, $\iota$ reindexed alongside). The writes touching keys are finite-map updates at named keys ($\sigma[k \mapsto v]$, $\sigma \smallsetminus k$, $\rho[k \mapsto r]$, $\iota[k \mapsto \iota(k) \oplus_k \nu]$, $\omega$'s domain $d_n$), all commuting with reindexing by $\chi_K$. No rule or operation computes a key, orders two keys, or reads $|{\cdot}|$ of a key set.

*Values.* No premise of any Table-1 row inspects a value: values occur only inside $\sigma$-tables and iterator payloads, and the only value-consuming positions are the operations' arguments and results (Defs. 23/24/29/31, Alg. 6), which pass values through or compare them by the coeffect's own $\simeq_k$ — an equivalence that the sort bijection is required to respect on classes (reserved value classes fixed; cf. §0.1). Writes place values into tables pointwise. So value bijections commute with every premise and write.

*Tags.* Tags occur only as the payload of $\mathsf{track}$/$\mathsf{app}$/$\mathsf{inv}$ and are never compared by any premise; they are carried through the accumulator as opaque payloads. Renaming commutes trivially.

Events and certificates: each event letter's payload is a tuple of carried identities, written from the same reads the premises make, so the emission at $\chi\cdot\gamma$ is the $\chi$-image; the certificate's $(\widehat s, \widehat a)$ is $\kappa$-normalized and hence *fixed* by $\chi$ (Lemma D2.0.6) — the certificate stream is $\chi$-invariant, which is what lets canonical experiments compare it across renamings. Well-formedness (Def. 58) is four conditions comparing names with names and keys with keys, preserved as in Lemma 56. $\square$

### 0.5 Frame lemma, packaged

**Lemma D2.0.9 (frame).** Let $r$ apply at $\gamma$ acting on $n$, and let $\gamma^+$ extend $\gamma$ by fibers and keys wholly outside $W_r(\gamma,n)$ in the following precise sense: every added fiber $m$ is vestigial (Lemma 57's sense: $\tau_m = \top$, $\theta_m = \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$, $\sigma_m = \varnothing$, no $\pi_{m'} = m$) or is a fiber none of whose fields occur in $W_r(\gamma,n)$'s reads — no key of $p_m$ or $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m)$ meets the row's key reads, $m$ is not a parent/child/reliance witness read by the row, and $m$'s activity does not change $\mathrm{target}_n$ — and every added key lies outside the row's key reads. Then $r$ applies at $\gamma^+$ acting on $n$ with the same $\kappa_r$-image, the same per-occurrence decisions under $f_r^{\mathbb S}$, and a result differing from $r$'s result at $\gamma$ exactly by the added material.

*Proof.* For vestigial additions this is Lemma 57(1) (a vestigial $n$ contributes to no observation a premise of a rule acting on $m \neq n$ reads: it is not Active so it enters no $\sigma_\gamma$; it provides no key, leaving $\gamma \vDash d_m$ and $\mathrm{target}_m$ unmoved; $\mathrm{installed}$ fails so it contributes no disjunct to $\mathrm{relied}_m$; it names no $\pi$; and $\theta,\tau,\pi$ are read only by rules acting on it), together with Lemma 54(1): no step acting on $n$ writes a table of $m \neq n$, so the addition survives untouched. For non-vestigial additions satisfying the stated separations, re-run Lemma 57's proof clause by clause: each premise of $r$'s row reads only the window fields (that is Definition D2.0.1, checked per rule in §1), and by hypothesis the added material meets none of them; the only two premises that relax rather than restrict under *removal* — O-Insert's freshness and disjointness (Lemma 57(2)) — here run in the extension direction and are excluded by the hypothesis that added fibers claim no key the row reads and are not the inserted name. The writes are confined to $n$ (Def. 48, via Lemma 54), so they commute with the extension. $\kappa_r$-equality is then Lemma D2.0.6 with $\chi = \mathrm{id}$ restricted along the window agreement. $\square$

## 1. The Factorization Lemma: $\mathbb S \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$

This section proves PAPER_REWRITE's Lemma 2 in full. The load is CF3, discharged rule-by-rule against Table 1 in §1.1–§1.10 and, for the operation content of iterator landings, in §1.11; §1.12 assembles CF1–CF7.

For each rule we give: **(W)** the window read off the row and its premises; **(K)** the occurrence decomposition $\kappa_r$, quoting the MASTER draft atoms we instantiate; **(G)** the proof that each premise is a Boolean function of the occurrence support; **(U)** the proof that the row's write is $\mathrm{lift}_r$ of per-occurrence updates, with $\widehat U_r$ enumerated; **(E)** emissions; **(Q)** equivariance and frame, by Lemmas D2.0.8/D2.0.9; **(C)** the no-cardinality check for the row: an explicit inventory of every symbol the row and its premises contain, verifying that none is an arithmetic or cardinality read. The check (C) is done separately for all ten rows, as MASTER requires.

Genuinely symmetric sub-arguments are written once in full at their first occurrence and afterwards cited with their delta: the *per-key view-relation decomposition* first appears at L-Iter (§1.5) and is reused at L-Finish, L-Divert, L-Leave; the *existential-witness fold* first appears at O-Insert (§1.1) and is reused at O-Remove (children) and L-Unload (reliance); the *per-key satisfaction product* appears at L-Begin (§1.4).

### 1.1 O-Insert

Row (Table 1): $\theta_n$ undefined $\to \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$, $\Psi^t = \mathrm{id}_\Gamma$, control fields edited: $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$. Premises: $n \notin \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$; $\pi \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma) \cup \{\mathsf{root}\}$; $(d,p,e) \in \mathfrak C_\Gamma$; $\forall m \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma).\ p \cap p_m = \varnothing$.

**(W)** Window: the requested name $n$ and, for freshness, the name set $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$ read *only through per-fiber name equalities* $[m = n]$; the requested parent $\pi$ and the per-fiber equalities $[m = \pi]$ plus the constant test $[\pi = \mathsf{root}]$; the component data $(d,p,e)$; and, for disjointness, for each present $m$ and each $k$, the memberships $[k \in p]$, $[k \in p_m]$. Frame: the (finite) index set of present fibers for the two folds; no order data.

**(K)** Per MASTER's draft — "atoms {parent-live?, ∃ declared-provision conflict (as singleton-key witness occurrence)}; freshness is system-controlled — mark interface-unrealizable" — $\kappa_{\text{O-Insert}}(W)$ contains: one scalar occurrence recording the atoms $\langle \mathsf{fresh?}, \mathsf{parent\mbox{-}live?}, \mathsf{comp\mbox{-}wf?} \rangle$ (the last: $(d,p,e) \in \mathfrak C_\Gamma$, a property of the submitted data alone) together with the equality pattern among $\{n, \pi, \mathsf{root}\}$ and the requested names; and one *conflict-witness occurrence* $\mathsf{conflict}(k\text{-pattern})$ for each pair $(m,k)$ with $k \in p \cap p_m$ — a singleton-key witness recording the key's equality pattern against $d \cup p$, not the identity of $m$ or the number of such pairs. Freshness is recorded as an atom but its false polarity is marked *interface-unrealizable*: names are system-drawn (Def. 44's discipline, PAPER.md Lemma 3's exclusion), so **(D1-D)** D1's Σ-table lists the $\mathsf{fresh?}{=}0$ vectors as unreachable at the interface, and no canonical experiment attempts them.

**(G)** Premise 1 is the negation of the existential $\exists m \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma).\ m = n$; each disjunct is one per-fiber equality; the premise holds iff the scalar atom $\mathsf{fresh?}$ — defined as the value of that fold — is $1$. It is a function of the occurrence support: the fold's value is recorded in the scalar occurrence, and no multiplicity enters (two fibers cannot share the name $n$, and even the *fold itself* only asks existence). Premise 2 is the disjunction $[\pi = \mathsf{root}] \vee \exists m.\ m = \pi$, likewise recorded as $\mathsf{parent\mbox{-}live?}$. Premise 3 reads the submitted data alone. Premise 4 — the representative *existential-witness fold*, written out once in full: the premise is $\neg\exists (m,k).\ m \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma) \wedge k \in p \wedge k \in p_m$. By construction of $\kappa$, the witness set of this existential is in bijection with the conflict-witness occurrences of $\kappa(W)$: an occurrence is placed *iff* a witness exists at its class, so

$$\text{premise 4 holds} \iff \mathrm{supp}(\kappa(W)) \text{ contains no occurrence of class } \mathsf{conflict}(\cdot).$$

This is a function of the support alone; in particular it does not depend on *how many* conflicting pairs exist — the guard is an absence test, insensitive to multiplicity. (This bijection between witnesses and witness occurrences is the single fact every reuse of this fold cites.)

**(U)** $\widehat U_{\text{O-Insert}} = \{\mathsf{new\mbox{-}entry}\}$: the write is the single registry extension $F[n \mapsto \langle d,p,e,\pi,\varnothing,\bot,\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)\rangle]$ — one entry, whose every field is either submitted data ($d,p,e,\pi$) or a reserved constant ($\varnothing, \bot, \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$). $\mathrm{lift}$ is the one-point registry union; there is no per-key or per-item content. $\Psi^t = \mathrm{id}_\Gamma$ per the row, so no state map runs.

**(E)** No ordinary event; on a false premise, the refusal event and refused-input log entry carrying the failing premise's reason $\varrho$ (Definition D2.0.4(1)).

**(Q)** Equivariance: Lemma D2.0.8 (its name part is Lemma 56's own case for O-Insert: freshness and the $\forall m.\ p \cap p_m$ premise compare names/keys by equality only). Frame: Lemma D2.0.9 — a fiber added outside the window must in particular claim no key of $p$ (else it *is* a conflict witness and lies inside the window), and then premises 1–4 and the one-point write are untouched.

**(C)** No-cardinality check for the row. The row's premises contain the symbols: $\notin$, $\in$, $\cup$, $\{\mathsf{root}\}$, $\mathfrak C_\Gamma$, $\forall$, $\cap$, $=\varnothing$. The conclusion contains: $[\,\cdot \mapsto \cdot\,]$ (registry extension) and the constant tuple. There is no $|{\cdot}|$, no order relation, no arithmetic operation, and no bound mentioning a number. The only "for all" quantifies over present fibers and is discharged as an absence-of-witness test. **Verified: this row is cardinality-free.**

### 1.2 O-Retire

Row: $\theta_n$ unconstrained $\to$ unchanged, $\Psi^t = \mathrm{id}_\Gamma$, edits $\tau_n$. Premise: $n \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$.

**(W)** Window: presence of $n$ (per-fiber name equalities $[m = n]$) and the field $\tau_n$ it writes. Frame: none.

**(K)** Per MASTER's draft — "trivial alphabet (unconditional); occurrence records the target's state class {pending, installed, failed} for write-aspect bookkeeping only" — one scalar occurrence: $\langle \mathsf{present?} \rangle$ plus the recorded state class $\mathrm{cls}(\theta_n) \in \{\mathsf{pending}, \mathsf{installed}, \mathsf{failed}\}$ (that is: $\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ / $\theta_n \neq \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$ / $\mathsf{Inactive}(\xi)$). The class is *not read by the guard* — O-Retire is unconditional on the fiber's state (p. 31: "O-Retire is unconditional on the fiber's state because retiring is a request") — it is carried so that the canonical experiments for the three classes exist separately and the verdict can pin the (identical) decision at each; this is bookkeeping in $\Sigma_r$, not a guard read, and CF3 permits reads of recorded atoms (they are inside $\Sigma_r$).

**(G)** The single premise is the existential presence fold, a function of the scalar atom $\mathsf{present?}$, exactly as in §1.1's premise 1 with polarity reversed. No other premise exists; in particular the guard is constant in $\mathrm{cls}(\theta_n)$ — which is itself the checkable content of the row ("unconditional") that the three per-class experiments witness.

**(U)** $\widehat U = \{\tau \mapsto \top\}$: one field assignment at one fiber. $\mathrm{lift}$ is trivial. **(E)** none / refusal if absent. **(Q)** Lemmas D2.0.8, D2.0.9 (the row reads and writes only $n$'s presence and $\tau_n$).

**(C)** Row symbols: $\in$, $[\tau_n \mapsto \top]$. No arithmetic, no cardinality, no order. **Verified: cardinality-free.**

### 1.3 O-Remove

Row: $\theta_n = \mathsf{Inactive}(-) \to$ undefined, $\Psi^t = \mathrm{id}_\Gamma$, edits $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$. Premises: $\tau_n = \top$; $\theta_n = \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$; $\forall m.\ \pi_m \neq n$.

**(W)** Window: $\tau_n$, the constructor of $\theta_n$, and for each present $m$ the equality $[\pi_m = n]$. Frame: the present-fiber index set of the childlessness fold.

**(K)** Per MASTER's draft — "atoms {τ, state = Inactive?, ∃ child (witness occurrence)}" — one scalar occurrence $\langle \tau_n{=}\top?,\ \theta_n{=}\mathsf{Inactive}(-)? \rangle$ and one *child-witness occurrence* per $m$ with $\pi_m = n$ (recording only that a child exists; identity forgotten to the equality pattern).

**(G)** Premises 1–2 are scalar pattern matches, recorded atoms. Premise 3 is the negative existential fold of §1.1's premise 4 with witness predicate $\pi_m = n$: it holds iff no child-witness occurrence is in the support. Function of the support; multiplicity (number of children) never read.

### 1.11 Operation clauses inside iterator landings

The landing classes $\mathsf{provide}$, $\mathsf{read}$, $\mathsf{setval}$, and the orchestration inputs $\mathsf{isolate}/\mathsf{intercept}$ route through the coeffect operations, whose defining equations are Defs. 23/24 (get/set), 28/29 (isolation), 30/31 (interception), and Algorithm 6 (proxy-mediated access). MASTER's draft: "per-operation small alphabets (own/foreign binding; realm same/joined/distinct; intercept chain depth-as-order-pattern up to 2; the three Alg-6 outcomes with ancestry-position class)". We verify that each defining equation factors through these occurrences and is cardinality-free; D1's Σ-table **(D1-A)** fixes the exact per-operation alphabets.

1. **$\mathsf{set}$/$\mathsf{provide}$ (Def. 23, eq. 21; Def. 29 eq. 28; Def. 31 eq. 30).** $\mathsf{set}(k,v)$ requires $k \notin \mathrm{dom}(\sigma)$ (fresh) and writes $\sigma[k \mapsto v]$ with inverse $\lambda\sigma'.\ \sigma' \smallsetminus k$; under isolation the precondition and write are transported along $\rho$ ($\rho(k) \notin \mathrm{dom}(\sigma)$, write at $\rho(k)$). The guard atom is the single membership — MASTER's classes $\mathsf{provide(fresh)}$ vs. $\mathsf{provide(conflict)}$ — a per-binding fact; the write and inverse touch the one binding ("Typing an operation of $k$ on $\mathcal V_k$ is what confines it to the binding at $k$: the lift reads and writes that binding and leaves every other key as it stands, so no side condition is needed to say so" — Def. 24's commentary). Function of the singleton occurrence; equation contains $\notin$, $[\cdot \mapsto \cdot]$, $\smallsetminus$ — no arithmetic.
2. **$\mathsf{get}$/operation application (Def. 23/24, eqs. 21–23).** $\mathsf{get}(k)$ requires $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma)$ and returns $\sigma(k)$; an operation $a \in \mathcal A_k$ acts on the value alone and lifts by eq. 23 to read and write the binding at $k$ and nothing else. Occurrence: the binding's presence and the *own/foreign* atom (whether $k \in d_n$ resp. $k \in p_n$ — the confinement classes Def. 48 reads). Per-binding; no arithmetic (the equations contain $\mathbf{let}$, application, $[\cdot\mapsto\cdot]$).
3. **$\mathsf{read}$ through Algorithm 6.** The resolution walks the fiber chain upward from the accessing context and takes the *first* of three exits: (a) $k \in \mathrm{fiber.committed}$ — return the committed binding ($\mathsf{rd}(n,k,v)$; classes $\mathsf{read(committed)}$ at the own fiber and $\mathsf{read(ancestor\mbox{-}committed)}$ at a proper ancestor — the *ancestry-position class*, an order-pattern over the finitely many chain positions the equality pattern distinguishes, not a depth count); (b) $k \in \mathrm{fiber.inject}$ without commitment — $\mathsf{rderr}(n,k,\mathsf{IA})$, class $\mathsf{read(declared\mbox{-}uncommitted)}$; (c) root reached — $\mathsf{rderr}(n,k,\mathsf{UD})$, class $\mathsf{read(undeclared)}$. Each loop iteration reads two memberships and one equality-with-root; the *outcome* is determined by the classes present along the chain and their relative order pattern, which the occurrence records as its ancestry-position class. The walk reads no count (its length is bounded by the parent tree, but no premise mentions that bound). Reading the view rather than the store (p. 60's commentary) is what makes this a window read of $\omega$-fields — consistent with §1.5's window. No arithmetic.
4. **$\mathsf{isolate}$ (Def. 29).** $\mathsf{isolate}(k,r)$ writes $\rho[k \mapsto r]$, deriving a context; no precondition ("a key already isolated is reassigned rather than refused"). Occurrence: the realm-relation atom — MASTER's *realm same/joined/distinct* — the equality pattern among $\rho(k)$, $r$, $k$. Single-binding write; no arithmetic.
5. **$\mathsf{intercept}$ (Def. 31).** $\mathsf{intercept}(k,\nu)$ writes $\iota[k \mapsto \iota(k) \oplus_k \nu]$; $\mathsf{get}(k,\mu)$ evaluates $\sigma(k)(\mu \oplus_k \iota(k))$. The monoid fold $\oplus_k$ is associative with unit $\epsilon_k$; the occurrence records the *order pattern* of the (at most two, per the drafted bound) merged metadata sources — component-declared and context-carried, right-biased — not their number: MASTER's "intercept chain depth-as-order-pattern up to 2" is the equality/order pattern of $\{\mu, \iota(k), \epsilon_k\}$. Where D1's analysis finds interface-reachable chains longer than the drafted pattern bound, that is a **Deviation** D1 must log against MASTER (§7, D-2 here flags the risk); the factorization itself is unaffected: $\oplus_k$ is a binary operator iterated by lift exactly as the accumulator composition of §1.5, and the per-occurrence content is one merge. No arithmetic in eqs. 29–30.

**Conclusion of §1.11.** Every landing's guard is a Boolean function of the operation occurrence(s); every write is a single-binding update or a lift-iterated binary fold; every equation-symbol inventory is free of arithmetic and cardinality reads. $\square$

## 2. The Transport Lemma

The lemma says exactly how far membership in $\mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ pins an implementation's behavior across configurations the suite never visited: per-occurrence decisions transport along $\kappa$-equality; whole-rule results transport along $\kappa$-equality *plus frame correspondence*. Remark D2.0.5 explains why the second half needs the extra hypothesis.

**Theorem D2.2 (Transport).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ with abstraction $A$, let $r \in \mathrm{Rules}$, and let $u, u' \in \mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I)$ with acting fibers $n, n'$. Write $W = W_r(A(u), n)$ and $W' = W_r(A(u'), n')$. Suppose there is a sort-preserving bijection $\chi$ with

$$\kappa_r(\chi \cdot W) = \kappa_r(W') \quad \text{as multisets over } \Sigma_r.$$

(By Lemma D2.0.6 the left side equals $\kappa_r(W)$, so the hypothesis is equivalently the bare multiset equality $\kappa_r(W) = \kappa_r(W')$; the bijection $\chi$ is nevertheless part of the data because the conclusion's correspondences are stated along it.) Then:

1. *(Enablement and refusal.)* $\mathbb I$'s $r$-tagged transition is enabled at $(u,n)$ iff at $(u',n')$, and when refused, refused with the same reason $\varrho$.
2. *(Per-occurrence updates and emissions.)* For every multiset bijection $\beta : \kappa_r(W) \to \kappa_r(W')$ matching equal occurrences, and every occurrence $s \in \kappa_r(W)$, the per-occurrence decision of $\mathbb I$ at $s$ in $W$ equals its decision at $\beta(s)$ in $W'$: same $\mathrm{Decision}_r$-value, hence the same normalized update constructor and the same emission constructors, with payloads corresponding under $\chi$'s equality-pattern action.
3. *(Whole-rule results.)* If moreover $\chi$ and some $\beta$ carry $W$'s combination frame to $W'$'s — same index-set correspondence and same order data (iterator position; accumulator composition order) — then the full abstract successor, certificate, and emitted word at $(u,n)$ are carried by $\chi$ to those at $(u',n')$, up to $\simeq/\approx$.

*Proof.* The quantification, stated pedantically once: the theorem quantifies over *all* pairs of reachable implementation configurations and acting fibers with $\kappa$-equal windows; $\chi$ acts on the abstract window $W$ (a tuple of abstract fields plus frame — §0.2) by its sortwise action on every carried name, key, value, and tag; nothing is assumed about the relation between $u$ and $u'$ as implementation states beyond reachability — that is the point.

(1) By CF3, $\mathbb I$'s enablement at $(u,n)$ is $\mathrm{lift}_r$'s guard formula (Definition D2.0.4(1)) evaluated on the family $\{f_r^{\mathbb I}(s)\}_{s \in \kappa_r(W)}$. The guard formula is a fixed Boolean combination depending only on which occurrence classes are present and their per-class decisions (§0.2, "guards factor through the support"). The multiset equality is used here through its consequence $\mathrm{supp}(\kappa_r(W)) = \mathrm{supp}(\kappa_r(W'))$: equal multisets have equal supports, and $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ — being a *function* on $\Sigma_r$, which is what CF3's factorization demands — assigns each class the same decision in both windows. Hence the two guard evaluations are literally the same Boolean computation on the same inputs, and agree; likewise the leftmost-failing-premise reason. Note what is *not* used: no relation between the concrete sizes, spellings, or layouts of $u$ and $u'$ — CF3's prohibition ("the table cannot inspect registry cardinality, allocation order, unnormalized spelling, or the number of irrelevant witnesses") is exactly what makes the occurrence multiset the whole input.

(2) Let $\beta$ be any multiset bijection matching equal occurrences (one exists by the hypothesis; there may be several when a class has multiplicity $> 1$, and the conclusion holds for each). For $s \in \kappa_r(W)$ we have $\beta(s) = s$ *as elements of $\Sigma_r$* (occurrences are normalized data; $\beta$ matches equal ones), so $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s) = f_r^{\mathbb I}(\beta(s))$ holds by reflexivity — the entire content of clause (2) is that $\mathbb I$ consults nothing else, which is CF3, plus the translation of the normalized payloads back to concrete identities: the update constructor's payload is an equality pattern over the occurrence's read identities, and $\chi$ carries $W$'s identities to $W'$'s matching ones by the hypothesis that $\chi\cdot W$ and $W'$ have equal $\kappa$-images at matched occurrences (Lemma D2.0.6(3): equality patterns determine, and are determined by, the identity constellation up to bijection). CF2 enters here: $\mathbb I$'s *concrete* per-occurrence behavior at $(u',n')$ is the $\chi$-image of its behavior at $(u,n)$ because renaming before the transition renames its result and events in the same way — without CF2, equal decisions on normalized occurrences could still be realized by non-corresponding concrete writes (e.g. an implementation writing a fixed hard-coded key when the pattern says "the read key"); equivariance excludes precisely that.

(3) By CF3, the full result at each window is $\mathrm{lift}_r$(frame, per-occurrence decisions) — with the *same, specified* $\mathrm{lift}_r$ at both windows (CF3 fixes the reconstruction operator; it is not per-implementation data). By (2) the decision families correspond under $(\chi,\beta)$; by the added hypothesis the frames correspond under the same pair. $\mathrm{lift}_r$'s three clauses are each invariant under such a correspondence: the guard formula by (1); the finite-map product because $\beta$ restricted to per-key occurrences is induced by $\chi_K$ on the index sets and the per-key supports are disjoint singletons; the order-sensitive folds because the order data correspond by hypothesis and the fold is the same binary operator applied in the corresponding order; the emission concatenation likewise. The certificate is $(r, \widehat s, \widehat a)$ with both components $\kappa$-normalized, hence *equal* (not just corresponding) at the two windows by Lemma D2.0.6 — which is the transport fact the verdicts rely on. The successor abstractions agree up to $\simeq/\approx$ because the assembled updates are equal as edits of corresponding fields (Lemma 55's conclusion paragraph gives the same-shape statement for $\mathbb S$; here it holds for $\mathbb I$'s *abstract image* by CF1's field preservation: $A$ carries the performed abstract update to the report-visible fields). $\square$

**Remark D2.2.1 (how the lemma is consumed).** In the safety proof (§4) the lemma is applied in the degenerate but crucial instance $W' = $ the window of the *specification* state matched to $A(u)$ by the induction hypothesis, $\chi$ the accumulated renaming, $\beta$ the identity on matched occurrences — there the frames correspond automatically (same abstract window up to $\simeq/\approx$, Lemma D2.0.6), so clause (3) applies and gives whole-rule agreement between $\mathbb I$'s step and $\mathbb S$'s. The genuinely two-implementation-states instance (clause (2) without (3)) is what licenses D1's canonical scripts to *stand for* every $\kappa$-equal configuration: the suite pins $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ at the canonical window, and transport carries the per-occurrence pin everywhere; the frame data at the deployment window is then handled by lift — checked once, at the accumulator-order and product tests (PAPER_REWRITE §5.3), not re-derived per configuration.

## 3. The Observation Reflection Lemma

Throughout this section fix $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$, a rule $r$, and a reachable signature $s \in \Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$ with its canonical experiment $(\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s}) \in T^\star$. Recall (PAPER_REWRITE §5.2) that $V_{r,s}$ requires equality, up to $\equiv$, of $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s})$ with the complete specification outcome set — equivalently: enablement or refusal equal to $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$; an equal normalized certificate; the prescribed ordinary event word; the prescribed normalized successor report after settling; termination whenever the specification terminates. By D1's Σ-table we use: **(D1-C)** $\rho_{r,s}$'s unique-up-to-$\equiv$ specification run reaches, after its prefix $\rho_0$, a pointed configuration $\gamma^\star$ with $s \in \kappa_r(W_r(\gamma^\star, n^\star))$, and its final action attempts $r$ there (directly for orchestration rules; by one lifecycle step followed by settling for lifecycle rules); **(D1-E)** the specification outcome set of $\rho_{r,s}$ is computed by exhaustive exploration of every fair choice, and is what $V_{r,s}$ compares against; **(D1-F)** every specification schedule of $\rho_{r,s}$ presents the same normalized signature $s$ at the attempt point (attempt-point determinacy).

**Theorem D2.3 (Reflection).** If $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s) \neq f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$, then $\mathbb I \not\models (\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s})$.

*Proof.* Suppose toward a contradiction that $\mathbb I \models (\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s})$, i.e. every run of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho_{r,s}$ is explored, non-divergent where the specification terminates, and $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s}) \equiv \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho_{r,s})$.

**Step 0 (a run of $\mathbb I$ reaches the attempt point).** The specification outcome set contains an outcome whose certificate word passes through the attempt point with signature $s$ (D1-C). Outcome-set equality in the direction $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S} \subseteq \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}$ (up to $\equiv$) yields a run $\pi$ of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho_{r,s}$ whose outcome $\equiv$-matches it; fix the sort-preserving bijection $\chi$ realizing the match. Along $\pi$, the certificate letters equal (after normalization, on which $\chi$ acts trivially — Lemma D2.0.6) the specification run's certificates; by CF4's faithfulness each certificate of $\mathbb I$ truthfully reports the occurrence its step consulted and the abstract update it performed, synchronously at the transition boundary — **[CF4 used]** — so at the position matching the attempt point, $\mathbb I$'s configuration $u^\star$ has $A(u^\star)$ presenting the signature $s$ at the acting fiber $\chi^{-1}(n^\star)$: the preceding certificates pin every abstract update made so far, and CF1's field preservation plus the report-free determinism of the abstract edit sequence give $A(u^\star) \simeq \chi^{-1}\cdot\gamma^\star$ (formally: induct along the prefix; at each step the certificate equality gives equal normalized $(\widehat s, \widehat a)$, and equal normalized updates applied to $\simeq$-related abstract states yield $\simeq$-related successors, Lemma 55's conclusion paragraph). By Lemma D2.0.6, $s \in \kappa_r(W_r(A(u^\star), \chi^{-1}n^\star))$.

**Step 1 (the deviant entry is used).** At $u^\star$ the script's final action attempts $r$. By CF3, $\mathbb I$'s decision there applies $f_r^{\mathbb I}$ pointwise on $\kappa_r(W_r(A(u^\star), \chi^{-1}n^\star))$ — in particular at $s$ — and combines with $\mathrm{lift}_r$. We case on the component of $\mathrm{Decision}_r$ at which $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s)$ and $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ differ. The cases are exhaustive over $\mathrm{Decision}_r$'s structure (Definition D2.0.3): enablement polarity; refusal reason; update constructor; emission constructors. Termination is handled in case (e).

**Case (a): enablement differs — $\mathbb S$ fires, $\mathbb I$ refuses (or is silent).** Every specification outcome of $\rho_{r,s}$ contains, at the attempt point, the certificate $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb S})$ with $\widehat a^{\mathbb S}$ the update component of $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ — *every* outcome, by attempt-point determinacy (D1-F) and because $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ is a function: no specification schedule can present a different $\widehat a$ at $\widehat s = s$. Now consider $\mathbb I$'s outcomes through $u^\star$ (nonempty by Step 0). If $r$ is an orchestration rule: $\mathbb I$ refuses the input, emitting a refusal event and logging it (CF4's synchrony makes the refusal, not some later compensation, the recorded observation — **[CF4 used]**); its outcome is $\mathsf{Refused}(\cdots)$ or contains the refusal letter, while no matching specification outcome refuses there (the specification premise is true at $s$ — that is what $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s) = \mathsf{fire}$ means, by Theorem D2.1's CF3 for $\mathbb S$); outcome unmatched. If $r$ is a lifecycle rule: $\mathbb I$ does not offer the $r$-step at $u^\star$; its runs from $u^\star$ either take other enabled steps or settle. In either case the outcome's word lacks the letter $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb S})$ at any position compatible with the matched prefix: a later occurrence of that certificate would require a later configuration presenting $s$ at the same fiber with $\mathbb I$ firing — but $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s)$ refuses *wherever* $s$ is presented (CF3: the table is a function of the occurrence alone; this is Transport, Theorem D2.2(1), doing its exact job). Since $\equiv$ preserves letter order and compares certificates after normalization (§0.1's convention), an $\mathbb I$-outcome missing a certificate letter that every specification outcome contains is matched by no specification outcome. Hence $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I} \not\equiv \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}$ — contradiction. The symmetric sub-case ($\mathbb S$ refuses, $\mathbb I$ fires) is written out only in its one asymmetric point, per the compression rule: $\mathbb I$'s outcome now *contains* a certificate $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \cdot)$ (or, for orchestration rules, lacks the refusal letter and log entry that every specification outcome carries — "no hidden acceptance") that no specification outcome contains at $\widehat s = s$; unmatched for the same order-preservation reason.

**Case (b): both fire, the update differs — $\widehat a^{\mathbb I} \neq \widehat a^{\mathbb S}$ at $s$.** $\mathbb I$'s step at the attempt point emits $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb I})$, truthfully by CF4 — **[CF4 used, and this is its load-bearing use: without faithfulness the implementation could perform the deviant update while certifying the specified one, and cases below show the event/report channels alone do not always catch it]**. As in case (a), *every* specification outcome's certificate at $\widehat s = s$ is $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb S})$, because $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ is a function and certificates are normalized (Lemma D2.0.6 kills renaming as an escape: $\widehat a$ is $\chi$-invariant). An outcome containing $\mathsf{cert}(r, s, \widehat a^{\mathbb I})$ with $\widehat a^{\mathbb I} \neq \widehat a^{\mathbb S}$ is therefore matched by no specification outcome. Contradiction.

**Case (c): both fire, updates agree, the emission differs** — a wrong, missing, extra, or reordered ordinary-event contribution (e.g. $\mathsf{app}$/$\mathsf{inv}$ letters, their order, a wrong $\mathsf{rd}$ payload class). Within one rule application the emission is a function of the decision family and frame ($\mathrm{lift}$ clause (3)); the deviation therefore appears in the emitted subword of the attempt step. Ordinary event order is preserved *exactly* by $\equiv$ (PAPER_REWRITE §3.1, with the stated intent: "LIFO recovery and provider-before-consumer ordering are temporal claims and must not disappear inside a state equivalence"). Could the deviant word coincide with a *different* specification schedule's word? Across schedules the specification varies only the interleaving of steps of distinct fibers, not the within-step contribution; and by **(D1-G)** the canonical script for an order-sensitive aspect pins a schedule-invariant distinguishing subword (single-fiber forcing, as in R5.1 where all three $\mathsf{inv}$ letters belong to one fiber's one L-Unload step). Hence the deviant outcome's word differs from every specification outcome's word at the first deviant letter position within that subword; unmatched. Contradiction.

**Case (d): refusal-reason difference (orchestration rules, both refuse).** The refused-input log is part of the quiescent report; CF1 preserves report fields and CF4 makes the log truthful — **[CF4 used]**. Two refusals with different reasons yield $\equiv$-different reports (reasons are normalized constants); every specification outcome carries the reason of $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$'s leftmost failing premise (Definition D2.0.4(1)); unmatched. Contradiction.

**Case (e): termination.** If, at or after the attempt, $\mathbb I$'s settle diverges while the specification's canonical run terminates (it always does — Theorem D2.5.7), then some run of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho_{r,s}$ yields $\mathsf{Diverge}$ or exceeds the scenario's proved transition bound, and Definition 5's clause (1)–(2) makes the test fail directly (PAPER_REWRITE §5.1: "a timeout or $\mathsf{Diverge}$ is a failure whenever the specification's canonical run terminates"). Contradiction.

The five cases cover every component in which the two $\mathrm{Decision}_r$ values can differ, so the assumption falls. $\square$

**Proposition D2.3.1 (Masking: reflection is *not* available in the certificate-free vocabulary).** Let $\mathrm{Out}^-$ denote outcomes with all certificate letters erased (the raw vocabulary of PAPER.md Definition 1: ordinary events, refusals, reports, termination). There exist $r$, $s \in \Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$, and a table $f^\sharp$ with $f^\sharp(s) \neq f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ such that the implementation $\mathbb I^\sharp$ obtained from $\mathbb S$ by replacing $f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ with $f^\sharp(s)$ (all else unchanged) satisfies: for *every* script $\rho$, $\mathrm{Out}^-_{\mathbb I^\sharp}(\rho) \equiv \mathrm{Out}^-_{\mathbb S}(\rho)$. Concretely: $r = $ L-Unload, $s$ the signature with scalar atoms $\langle \theta{=}\mathsf{Unloading},\ \mathrm{cls}(\zeta) = \bot,\ \tau_n = \top,\ \text{post-target} = \bot \rangle$, no reliance witness, and an accumulator item of class $\mathsf{provide}^{-1}$; $f^\sharp(s)$ replaces that item's $\mathsf{apply\mbox{-}inverse}(\mathsf{provide}^{-1})$ by $\mathsf{skip}$.

*Proof.* $\mathbb I^\sharp$ deviates only in the store write of the retirement-unload's provide-inverse: after its L-Unload at a retired fiber $n$, the residue $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_n)$ survives where the specification has $\sigma_n \smallsetminus k$. We show no certificate-free observation, on any script, ever differs. (i) *Events:* an item of class $\mathsf{provide}^{-1}$ emits no ordinary letter (§1.10(E) — $\mathcal E$ has no un-provide letter), so the emitted words of the deviant and specified steps are equal. (ii) *Reports:* the store report is $\mathrm{st}(k) = \sigma_\gamma(k)$, and $\sigma_\gamma$ unions the tables of *Active* fibers only (eq. 40); after the unload $n$ is $\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$, so $\sigma_n$ — with or without residue — contributes nothing; lifecycle classes and outcomes are untouched. (iii) *Future behavior:* the residue could matter only through a later read of $\sigma_n$. By Lemma 55's premise inventory, no premise of any rule reads $\sigma_m(k)$ of a non-Active $m$ (premises reach $\sigma$ only through $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_\gamma)$ and $\mathrm{provider}$); an effect function's reads are confined by Def. 48(2) to tables *through the fiber's own declared window*, again via the Active union or the committed view (Alg. 6 reads views, not stores). The one path back would be a re-activation of $n$, whose effects run against $\sigma_n$; but $s$ has $\tau_n = \top$, $\tau$ is monotone (Lemma 54(5): written only at $\top$), so $\mathrm{target}_n = \bot$ forever (Def. 46) and L-Begin never fires at $n$ again. O-Remove's premises read $\tau_n$, $\theta_n$, and parenthood — not $\sigma_n$ — and removal erases the residue. Hence by induction along any continuation, every reachable pair of states (deviant vs. specified) differs only in the dead field $\sigma_n$ of the permanently retired, non-Active $n$, which no premise, event, or report ever reads. (iv) *Termination:* the deviant table changes no guard, so the step relation's shape — and Theorem D2.5.7's argument — is unchanged. So all certificate-free outcome sets agree. $\square$

**Consequences — stated prominently, and carried to the main theorem.**

1. **The theorem's vocabulary is the certificate-instrumented one.** Theorem D2.3, and therefore Theorem D2.6, hold for the observation vocabulary of PAPER_REWRITE §2.3, in which certificates are letters of $w$ and $V_{r,s}$ compares them. This is not a technicality: by Proposition D2.3.1 the biconditional in the certificate-*free* vocabulary would require *weakening both sides the same way* — and it does hold there in weakened form: the deviant $\mathbb I^\sharp$ of the proposition also *conforms* in the certificate-free reading of $\mathbf{conf}$ (its outcome sets match $\mathbb S$'s on every script), so it is a counterexample to neither direction. The calculus is *self-healing* at the masked entries (PAPER.md Remark 2's phenomenon, here proved rather than reported): a decision difference there is unobservable because it is semantically inert. What is genuinely lost without certificates is only the slogan "passing determines the table" (PAPER_REWRITE Lemma 5) — table equality holds on the *observation-relevant quotient* of $\mathrm{Decision}_r$, not on the nose.
2. **Certificate-adequacy obligation (sharpening CF4; see §7, correction (C4)).** For Theorem D2.3's case (b) to have force, the certificate component $\widehat a$ must actually expose the per-occurrence update at every entry — including σ-writes on fibers that are not Active at report time (the masked entries above). CF4 must therefore be read (and audited) as: *the adapter's $\widehat a$ ranges over all of $\widehat U_r$, including update aspects invisible to events and reports*. An adapter that derives certificates from the event/report stream alone cannot discharge CF4 at the masked entries; Proposition D2.3.1 is the proof.

## 4. The safety direction

**Lemma D2.4.1 (table equality; PAPER_REWRITE Lemma 5, full).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ and $\mathbb I \models T^\star$. Then $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s) = f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ for every $r \in \mathrm{Rules}$ and every $s \in \Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$.

*Proof.* Fix $(r,s)$ with $s$ reachable. By D1's Σ-table **(D1-C)**, $T^\star$ contains the canonical experiment $(\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s})$ — this is the surjectivity of the canonical-realization table onto reachable signatures (PAPER_REWRITE Lemma 4, whose construction D1 executes). $\mathbb I \models T^\star$ gives $\mathbb I \models (\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s})$; the contrapositive of Theorem D2.3 gives $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s) = f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$. $\square$

### 4.1 Behaviors, matched prefixes, deviations, and the well-order

**Definition D2.4.2 (tagged executions and matched prefixes).** A *tagged execution* of $\mathbb I$ on a script $\rho$ is a finite sequence $e = u_0 \xrightarrow{\ell_1} u_1 \xrightarrow{\ell_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{\ell_m} u_m$ of implementation transitions from the initial state, where each $\ell_i$ is, per CF1's complete tagging, either an orchestration input of $\rho$ (accepted or refused), a lifecycle tag $(r_i, n_i)$, or an *untagged* internal step (which by CF1 changes no abstract field and emits nothing). A specification execution $d = \gamma_0 \to \cdots \to \gamma_{m'}$ *matches* $e$ *up to $j$ under $(\chi, \cdot)$* when there is a sort-preserving bijection $\chi$ and a monotone surjection from the tagged steps among $\ell_1..\ell_j$ onto $d$'s steps such that corresponding steps carry $\chi$-corresponding labels (same rule, $\chi$-related fiber; same input verdict), $\equiv$-equal emissions and certificates, and $\gamma_i \simeq \chi^{-1}\cdot A(u_{\iota(i)})$ at every corresponding pair, with untagged steps of $e$ matched to nothing.

**Definition D2.4.3 (deviation; the well-order).** A *deviation of type F (forward)* is a triple $(\rho, e, j)$ where $e$ is a tagged execution of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho$, its first $j-1$ steps are matched by some specification execution, but no specification execution matches its first $j$ steps — including the sub-cases: step $j$'s tag/decision/update/emission/certificate/refusal disagrees with every $\mathbb S$-continuation; step $j$ is an untagged step that changes an abstract field or emits (excluded outright by CF1, listed for exhaustiveness); or $u_j$ is quiescent-reported with a report no matched $\mathbb S$-quiescent state's report $\equiv$-equals. A *deviation of type B (backward)* is a triple $(\rho, e, j)$ where $e$'s first $j$ steps are matched by a specification execution $d$, and some transition of $\mathbb S$ enabled at $d$'s endpoint (an accepted input of $\rho$'s next action, or an enabled lifecycle rule during a settle) has no correspondingly labeled transition or refusal offered by $\mathbb I$ at $u_j$. Well-order: deviations are ordered by their index $j \in \mathbb N$; $(\mathbb N, \le)$ is a well-order, so if any deviation (of either type) exists, one with minimal $j$ exists. (The script $\rho$ and execution $e$ at the minimum are not unique and need not be; minimality of $j$ is all the argument uses. We do not order by execution length: the steps after $j$ are irrelevant, and truncating at $j$ keeps $j$ fixed.)

**Theorem D2.4 (safety).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ and $\mathbb I \models T^\star$. Then no deviation of either type exists. Consequently, for every script $\rho$: every finite tagged execution of $\mathbb I$ on $\rho$ is matched in full by a specification execution (every finite behavior of $\mathbb I$ is one of $\mathbb S$), and every finite specification execution is matched by an offered execution of $\mathbb I$.

*Proof.* Suppose a deviation exists and fix one, $(\rho, e, j)$, with $j$ minimal, of type F or B. By minimality, the first $j-1$ steps of $e$ (type F) resp. $j$ steps (type B) are matched; fix a matching specification execution $d$ with endpoint $\gamma$ and renaming $\chi$, so that

$$\gamma \;\simeq\; \chi^{-1}\cdot A(u_{j-1}) \quad (\text{type F, writing } u_{j-1} \text{ for the state step } j \text{ leaves from; type B: } \gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}\cdot A(u_j)).$$

**The reachability bridge — spelled out.** The deviating configuration is reached by $\mathbb I$, and $\kappa_r$, $\Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$, and $T^\star$ are all defined over *specification* windows. Three facts bridge the gap, and each is genuinely needed. (i) CF1's abstraction is total on $\mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I)$, so $A(u_{j-1})$ *exists* as an abstract registry — without CF1 the deviating state would have no signature to speak of. (ii) $A(u_{j-1})$ by itself need **not** be $\mathbb S$-reachable: CF1 constrains $A$'s totality and field preservation, not its image. It is the induction hypothesis — the matched prefix — that supplies $\mathbb S$-reachability: $\gamma$ is the endpoint of a genuine specification execution from the empty registry, hence reachable, and $\gamma \simeq \chi^{-1} \cdot A(u_{j-1})$. (iii) Lemma D2.0.6 transports signatures across exactly this gap: for any rule $r$ and fiber $n$,

$$\kappa_r\big(W_r(A(u_{j-1}), n)\big) = \kappa_r\big(W_r(\gamma, \chi^{-1}n)\big),$$

and the right-hand window sits at a reachable specification state, so **(D1-B)** every occurrence in it lies in $\Sigma_r^{\mathrm{reach}}$. This is the only point in the whole development where membership of the *implementation's* current occurrences in the *reachable* alphabet is established, and it holds only because steps $< j$ agreed; a deviation at an earlier step could have marooned $A(u)$ at an abstract state outside $\mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb S)$ whose occurrences $T^\star$ never pinned. Minimality is thus not bookkeeping — it is the hypothesis that keeps the table applicable.

**Type F, step $j$ tagged $(r,n)$ (lifecycle) or an input of constructor $r$ (orchestration).** By the bridge, every occurrence $s \in \kappa_r(W_r(A(u_{j-1}), n))$ is reachable; by Lemma D2.4.1, $f_r^{\mathbb I}(s) = f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)$ at each. Apply Theorem D2.2 in the instance of Remark D2.2.1: $W = W_r(A(u_{j-1}), n)$, $W' = W_r(\gamma, \chi^{-1}n)$, the frames corresponding under $(\chi, \mathrm{id})$ because the windows are $\simeq$-related images of one another (Lemma D2.0.6 gives both the multiset equality and the frame correspondence). Clause (3) plus table equality yields: $\mathbb I$'s decision, refusal reason, assembled abstract update, emitted word, and certificate at step $j$ are carried by $\chi$ to exactly $\mathbb S$'s at $(\gamma, \chi^{-1}n)$ — where "$\mathbb S$'s" means the result of $f_r^{\mathbb S}$-with-$\mathrm{lift}_r$, which by Theorem D2.1 (CF3 for $\mathbb S$) *is* the specification's transition relation at that state. Moreover the successor abstractions re-relate: $A(u_j)$'s abstract update equals the specification successor's up to $\simeq/\approx$ (Theorem D2.2(3)), so $d$ extended by the corresponding $\mathbb S$-step matches $e$'s first $j$ steps. That contradicts "$(\rho,e,j)$ is a type-F deviation". The sub-cases fall with it: a tag/decision disagreement is excluded by the decision equality; an emission/certificate disagreement by the word equality; a refusal disagreement by the reason equality.

**Type F, step $j$ untagged.** By CF1 an untagged step changes no abstract field and emits nothing, so it cannot make the prefix unmatched (Definition D2.4.2 matches untagged steps to nothing and compares only abstract data); this sub-case is empty.

**Type F, report mismatch at quiescence.** $u_j = u_{j-1}$ quiescent-reported. Reports are functions of the abstract state's report fields (CF1's preservation clause) and $\gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}\cdot A(u_{j-1})$; $\simeq$-related states have $\equiv$-equal reports (eq. 53 keeps every report-visible field up to the value equivalences $\equiv$ absorbs; the refused-input log agrees by the matched prefix's refusal agreement). And $\gamma$ is *specification*-quiescent iff $A(u_{j-1})$ is (quiet is a function of the fields $\simeq$ preserves, Lemma 55's premise inventory applied to eq. 45); $\mathbb I$'s settle returns exactly at abstract quiescence by CF6. So the reports match; sub-case empty.

**Type B.** Let the unmatched enabled $\mathbb S$-transition at $\gamma$ be an application of $r$ at $\chi^{-1}n$ (or an accepted input). By Theorem D2.1 (CF3 for $\mathbb S$), its enablement means $\mathrm{lift}_r$'s guard passes on $\{f_r^{\mathbb S}(s)\}$ over $\kappa_r(W_r(\gamma, \chi^{-1}n))$. By the bridge and Lemma D2.4.1 the same guard passes on $\{f_r^{\mathbb I}(s)\}$ over the $\kappa$-equal window at $A(u_j)$, so by CF3 $\mathbb I$'s $r$-transition at $(u_j, n)$ is enabled — i.e. *offered*: CF3's enablement is the implementation's transition relation, and PAPER_REWRITE §3.2's reading ("an implementation of the calculus must offer every enabled calculus transition, even if its production scheduler deterministically selects one of them in a particular execution") makes offering, not scheduling, the content of the clause; fairness (CF6) separately guarantees the offer is realized in some explored run, which is what $\mathrm{Out}$-set claims need (§6). With the offer present and (by the type-F argument) agreeing in decision, update, and emission, the $\mathbb S$-transition is matched. Contradiction.

Both types are refuted, so no deviation exists. The two consequences restate the definitions: absence of type-F deviations at every $j$ gives, by induction on $j$ (the matching of Definition D2.4.2 extends step by step), a full match for every finite tagged execution; absence of type-B deviations gives the converse offering. $\square$

## 5. The termination direction

Safety (§4) matches every *finite* behavior. This section rules out the remaining escape: an implementation that agrees on all finite prefixes but settles forever (or stops settling early), and — on the specification side — establishes that $\mathbb S$ itself never diverges on the test language, *including* the $\prec$-cyclic scenarios outside Thm. 66's hypotheses. We absorb and generalize PAPER.md §5.3's M4 derivation.

### 5.1 Untagged stuttering is excluded

**Interpretive convention (CF6, exact settling).** We read CF6's clause "settle terminates exactly when a quiescent state is reached" as the conjunction: (i) if a settle's run reaches a state $u$ with $A(u)$ quiescent (eq. 45), the settle returns at $u$ after finitely many further (necessarily untagged, abstractly irrelevant) steps; (ii) a settle does not return at any $u$ with $A(u)$ not quiescent. This is the only reading under which the clause is checkable against the abstraction, and it is logged as an interpretation, not a change (§7, D-1).

**Lemma D2.5.1 (stutter exclusion).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ with $\mathbb I \models T^\star$, and let a fair settle of $\mathbb I$ produce an infinite run $u_0 \to u_1 \to \cdots$. Then the run contains infinitely many *tagged* steps.

*Proof.* Suppose only finitely many steps are tagged; let $u_N$ follow the last one. Every step after $N$ is untagged, hence (CF1's tagging totality: "there are no untagged transitions that change an abstract field or emit a P1 event") changes no abstract field: $A(u_t)$ is constant $= A(u_N)$ for $t \ge N$ up to the identity on abstract fields. Two cases on $A(u_N)$. If $A(u_N)$ is quiescent: by CF6(i) the settle returns after finitely many further steps — contradicting infinitude of the run *as a settle run*. If $A(u_N)$ is not quiescent: since steps $\le N$ form a finite tagged execution, Theorem D2.4 matches it by a specification execution with endpoint $\gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}A(u_N)$, reachable and non-quiescent; by Theorem D2.5.7(1) below (no deadlock, general form) some lifecycle rule is enabled at $\gamma$, hence — by the type-B argument of Theorem D2.4 — offered by $\mathbb I$ at $u_N$ and at every later $u_t$ (the abstract state, hence the guard evaluation, is constant along the untagged tail). CF6's fairness among enabled lifecycle rules then forces the offer to be taken after finitely many steps — a tagged step after $N$. Contradiction. $\square$

### 5.2 Two auxiliary invariants

Both are stated for $\mathbb S$; through CF1+Theorem D2.4 they transfer to $A$-images of matched executions. "Reachable" means reached from the empty registry by any finite step sequence (orchestration and lifecycle, Def. 53), which by Thm. 59 is well formed (Def. 58).

**Lemma D2.5.2 (ω-provenance).** At every reachable $\gamma$: for all $n$ with $\mathrm{installed}_n(\gamma)$ and all $k \in d_n$, the resolver $m := \omega_n(k)$ satisfies $m \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$, $\mathrm{installed}_m(\gamma)$, and $k \in p_m$.

*Proof.* The first two conjuncts are Def. 58(3)–(4), maintained by Thm. 59. The third, by induction along the history. It holds vacuously at $\gamma^0$. Steps preserving it: $\omega_n$ comes into existence only at an L-Begin of $n$ (Lemma 54(2)), which writes $\omega_n = \mathrm{target}_n(\gamma^t) \neq \bot$, whose value at $k$ is $\mathrm{provider}_k(\gamma^t)$ — an Active fiber $m$ with $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$ (eq. 40 and Def. 43's $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_n) \subseteq p_n$); $p_m$ is never written after $m$'s entry (Lemma 54(5)), so the conjunct persists while $\omega_n$ does; $\omega_n$ is constant throughout the episode (Lemma 54(2)) and ceases at L-Unload, after which the claim for $n$ is vacuous until the next L-Begin; O-Remove cannot remove $m$ while $n$ is installed (its premise $\theta_m = \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$ plus Def. 58(4): $\mathrm{installed}_n \wedge \omega_n(k) = m \Rightarrow \mathrm{installed}_m$, so the premise fails); no other step touches any field named. $\square$

**Lemma D2.5.3 (Frozen-Cycle).** Let $\gamma$ be reachable and let $C = \{n_1, \dots, n_c\}$ ($c \ge 1$, indices mod $c$) be a $\prec$-cycle within $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$: $n_i \prec n_{i+1}$ for all $i$, i.e. (Def. 65) there are keys $k_{i+1} \in p_{n_i} \cap d_{n_{i+1}}$. Then at $\gamma$: no member of $C$ is installed, and every member has $\mathrm{target}_{n_i}(\gamma) = \bot$; consequently no lifecycle rule applies at any member (L-Begin's premise $\mathrm{target} \neq \bot$ fails; every other lifecycle rule's premise $\theta \neq \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$ fails), and each member satisfies its $\mathrm{quiet}$ clause (eq. 45, first line, via $\mathrm{target} = \bot$).

*Proof.* *Self-loops ($c = 1$; $k \in p_n \cap d_n$) first, as the base the general propagation cannot handle.* Suppose $n$ is installed at some reachable state; by Lemma 54(4) there is a first step in its history making it installed, an L-Begin of $n$ at some $\gamma^t$, whose premise gives $\gamma^t \vDash d_n$, so $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_{\gamma^t})$: some Active $m$ has $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$. Since $k \in p_n$ and both $m, n \in \mathrm{dom}(F_{\gamma^t})$, Def. 58(2) forces $m = n$; but $\theta_n = \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ at its own L-Begin, not Active. Contradiction; $n$ is never installed at any reachable state, and $\mathrm{target}_n = \bot$ at $\gamma$ because $k$'s only possible present provider is $n$ itself (Def. 58(2) again), which is not Active.

*Cycles with $c \ge 2$.* Fix the history $\gamma^0 \to \cdots \to \gamma^T = \gamma$ and let $[t_0, T]$ be the maximal interval with all of $C$ present throughout ($t_0$ exists: all are present at $T$; presence changes only at O-Insert/O-Remove).

*Claim 1: no member is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$.* The step into $\gamma^{t_0}$ is an O-Insert (orchestrator's or a registration's) of some member $n_\ell$ — at $t_0 - 1$ some member was absent and at $t_0$ all are present, and only O-Insert adds presence — and its conclusion gives $\theta_{n_\ell} = \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ at $t_0$: $n_\ell$ is not installed. (If $t_0 = 0$ the registry is empty and the claim is vacuous, but $C \neq \varnothing$ forces $t_0 > 0$.) Now the *instantaneous propagation*: suppose some $n_i$ is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$. Then $k_i \in d_{n_i}$, and by Lemma D2.5.2 the resolver $m = \omega_{n_i}(k_i)$ is present, installed, with $k_i \in p_m$. Also $k_i \in p_{n_{i-1}}$ and $n_{i-1}$ is present (co-presence). By Def. 58(2), two present fibers with a common provision key are equal: $m = n_{i-1}$. So $n_{i-1}$ is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$. Iterating around the cycle ($c$ applications), *every* member is installed at $\gamma^{t_0}$ — including $n_\ell$. Contradiction. 

*Claim 2: "no member installed" is preserved on $[t_0, T]$.* Suppose it holds at $\gamma^{t}$ ($t_0 \le t < T$) and fails at $\gamma^{t+1}$. By Lemma 54(4) the step is an L-Begin of some $n_i$, whose premise $\mathrm{target}_{n_i}(\gamma^t) \neq \bot$ gives $\gamma^t \vDash d_{n_i}$, so $k_i$ has an Active — hence installed — provider $m$ at $\gamma^t$ with $k_i \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$; as in Claim 1, $m = n_{i-1}$ (both present on the interval), so $n_{i-1}$ is installed at $\gamma^t$ — contradicting the hypothesis at $t$. 

Claims 1–2 give "no member installed at $\gamma$". Finally $\mathrm{target}_{n_i}(\gamma) = \bot$: $k_i$'s possible present providers at $\gamma$ are fibers $m$ with $k_i \in p_m$; $n_{i-1}$ is one, and any other present one would violate Def. 58(2) against $n_{i-1}$; so the only candidate is $n_{i-1}$, not Active (not even installed); hence $k_i \notin \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_\gamma)$, $\neg(\gamma \vDash d_{n_i})$, and Def. 46 gives $\bot$. $\square$

**Lemma D2.5.4 (Frozen-Cone).** Let $\gamma$ be reachable and let $c = m_0 \prec m_1 \prec \cdots \prec m_j = n$ be a $\prec$-path with all $m_i \in \mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$ and $m_0$ on a $\prec$-cycle within $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$. Then $n$ is not installed at $\gamma$ and $\mathrm{target}_n(\gamma) = \bot$; hence (as in Lemma D2.5.3) no lifecycle rule applies at $n$ and $n$'s quiet clause holds.

*Proof.* Induction on $j$; $j = 0$ is Lemma D2.5.3. Step: assume $m_{j-1}$ is not installed at $\gamma$ (IH — note the IH is *instantaneous at $\gamma$*, which suffices because every inference below reads $\gamma$ alone). Let $k \in p_{m_{j-1}} \cap d_n$ witness $m_{j-1} \prec n$. If $n$ were installed at $\gamma$, Lemma D2.5.2 would give a present installed resolver $m = \omega_n(k)$ with $k \in p_m$; both $m$ and $m_{j-1}$ present with $k \in p_m \cap p_{m_{j-1}}$ forces $m = m_{j-1}$ (Def. 58(2)) — installed, contradicting the IH. And $\mathrm{target}_n(\gamma) = \bot$ exactly as in Lemma D2.5.3's last paragraph, with $m_{j-1}$ in the role of $n_{i-1}$. $\square$

### 5.3 The per-fiber and attribution bounds, restated and reproved

Thm. 66's proof establishes two counting claims, (A) and (B), inside its own text; since we must use them outside the theorem's acyclicity hypothesis, we restate and reprove them here rather than cite proof-internal steps.

**Lemma D2.5.5 (per-fiber bound; Thm. 66's (A), acyclicity-free).** Let a lifecycle-only step sequence be given with $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$, and let $[a,b]$ be a maximal interval of it on which $\mathrm{target}^t_n$ is constant, $= \omega^*$. Then at most $K + 4$ steps in $[a,b]$ act on $n$.

*Proof.* Case on $\theta_n^a$, reading Table 1's $\theta$-columns; every transition of $n$ rewrites $\theta_n$ (fifth column), so the steps acting on $n$ in the interval trace a path in the state diagram (Fig. 2) whose edges are the rules, and it suffices to bound the longest path consistent with a constant target.

*From $\mathsf{Active}(-,\omega)$ with $\omega \neq \omega^*$:* the only rule at Active is L-Leave (its premise $\mathrm{target} \neq \omega$ holds); then at $\mathsf{Unloading}$ only L-Unload (L-Unload has no target premise); this lands in $\mathsf{Inactive}(\zeta)$. If $\omega^* = \bot$ or $\zeta = \xi$, no rule applies at $n$ any more within the interval (L-Begin needs $\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ and $\mathrm{target} \neq \bot$): $2$ steps. Otherwise one L-Begin (writing the committed view $\omega^*$ — the target itself, so no later rule sees disagreement), then at most $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$ landings (L-Iter at most $K-1$ times then L-Finish, since each landing consumes one iteration of $e_n$ and the continuation chain has length $\le K$; or an L-Raise at some landing), and — only on the L-Raise branch — one further L-Unload into $\mathsf{Inactive}(\xi)$, after which nothing applies. Within the interval no L-Divert or L-Leave can fire after the L-Begin: their premises need $\mathrm{target} \neq \omega$, but the committed view written was $\omega^*$ and the target is constant at $\omega^*$. Total $\le 2 + 1 + K + 1 = K + 4$.

*From $\mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega)$ with $\omega \neq \omega^*$:* an L-Divert (or L-Raise) replaces the L-Leave above; the remainder is a suffix of the same path: $\le K + 4$. *From $\mathsf{Active}(-,\omega^*)$, $\mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega^*)$, $\mathsf{Unloading}$, $\mathsf{Inactive}$:* each is a state of the path above, and the reachable continuation within the interval is a suffix of it: $\le K + 4$. (At $\mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ with $\omega^* = \bot$, and at $\mathsf{Inactive}(\xi)$, zero steps.) $\square$

**Lemma D2.5.6 (turn attribution; Thm. 66's (B), acyclicity-free form).** In a lifecycle-only step sequence, if $\mathrm{target}^t_n \neq \mathrm{target}^{t+1}_n$ and step $t$ acts on $m$, then $m \prec n$ (possibly $m = n$ only when $n \prec n$) or step $t$ writes $\tau_n$; and the $\tau_n$ case occurs at most once in the whole sequence.

*Proof.* By Def. 46, $\mathrm{target}_n$ is a function of $\tau_n$ and of the tables of the Active fibers at the keys of $d_n$ (through $\gamma \vDash d_n$ and $\mathrm{provider}_k$, $k \in d_n$). If the value changes at step $t$, one of those inputs changed. $\tau_n$: Table 1's fifth column writes $\tau$ only at O-Retire — within a lifecycle-only sequence, only as the inverse a registration's accumulator or an $\mathsf{register}^{-1}$ item takes (Def. 47) — and $\tau$ is monotone, written only at $\top$ (Lemma 54(5)), so it turns at most once ever; that is the "one turn $\tau_n$ affords". Otherwise some $k \in d_n$ changed provision status: either $\sigma_m$ changed at a fiber $m$ with $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \cup$ (post-step) $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m)$ — by Lemma 54(1) tables change only at the acting fiber, so $m$ is the acting fiber, and $\mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$ gives $k \in p_m \cap d_n$, i.e. $m \prec n$ — or $m$'s Active-membership changed, i.e. $\theta_m$ was written, which happens only at steps acting on $m$ (fifth column), with the same $k \in p_m \cap d_n$ conclusion ($m$ was or becomes the Active provider of $k$, and providers satisfy $k \in \mathrm{dom}(\sigma_m) \subseteq p_m$). A registration's O-Insert adds a fresh non-Active fiber and moves no target (its entry provides nothing yet, $\sigma = \varnothing$). $\square$

### 5.4 Generalized progress

**Theorem D2.5.7 (progress without acyclicity).** Let $\gamma^\dagger$ be reachable, let every fiber satisfy $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$, and let the set $N$ of names ever present along any lifecycle-only continuation be finite (both hold for the test language: scripts and component payloads are finite data, so iteration counts and the registration tree are structurally bounded — the payload nesting of $\mathsf{register}$ steps is a finite tree with branching $\le$ script length). Then:

1. *(No deadlock.)* If $\neg\mathrm{quiet}(\gamma)$ at a state $\gamma$ reachable from $\gamma^\dagger$ by lifecycle steps, some lifecycle rule applies at $\gamma$.
2. *(Termination.)* Every maximal lifecycle-only step sequence from $\gamma^\dagger$ is finite.

Consequently every settle of $\mathbb S$ ends, after finitely many steps, in a quiescent state. **No $\prec$-acyclicity is assumed**; this strengthens Thm. 66 on exactly the axis PAPER.md §5.3's M4 derivation handled ad hoc, and contains that derivation as the special case "two mutually-declaring fibers, both frozen by Lemma D2.5.3, state already quiescent".

*Proof.* (1) Let $n$ violate its quiet clause (eq. 45). Reading Table 1 against the four kinds, exactly as in Thm. 66's no-deadlock case analysis (which nowhere uses acyclicity): if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Inactive}(\bot)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n \neq \bot$, L-Begin applies; if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega_n)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n = \omega_n$, whichever of L-Iter, L-Finish, L-Raise the iterator's value selects applies; if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Reloading}(-,-,\omega_n)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n \neq \omega_n$, L-Raise (if the iteration raises) or L-Divert applies; if $\theta_n = \mathsf{Active}(-,\omega_n)$ with $\mathrm{target}_n \neq \omega_n$, L-Leave applies. If no fiber is of these kinds, some $m_0$ has $\theta_{m_0} = \mathsf{Unloading}(-,-,-)$. Construct $m_0, m_1, \dots$: given $m_j$ in Unloading, either $\neg\mathrm{relied}_{m_j}$ — L-Unload applies, stop — or there are $m_{j+1} \neq m_j$, $k_j \in d_{m_{j+1}}$ with $\mathrm{installed}_{m_{j+1}}$ and $\omega_{m_{j+1}}(k_j) = m_j$; by Lemma D2.5.2, $k_j \in p_{m_j}$, so $m_j \prec m_{j+1}$; and $m_{j+1}$, being installed and (by the standing case assumption) of none of the four kinds above, is itself in Unloading, so the construction continues. If the construction never stopped, finiteness of $\mathrm{dom}(F_\gamma)$ would force a repetition, exhibiting a $\prec$-cycle $\{m_i, \dots, m_{j}\}$ all of whose members are *installed and co-present at $\gamma$* — contradicting Lemma D2.5.3. (This replaces Thm. 66's appeal to acyclicity: the chain is $\prec$-increasing *and consists of installed fibers*, and installed fibers span no cycle.) So the construction stops, and a rule applies.

(2) Suppose some maximal lifecycle-only sequence from $\gamma^\dagger$ is infinite. $N$ is finite and every step acts on some name, so some fiber $n_{(0)}$ takes infinitely many steps. By Lemma D2.5.5, an interval of constant $\mathrm{target}_{n_{(0)}}$ contains $\le K+4$ of them, so $\mathrm{target}_{n_{(0)}}$ turns infinitely often. By Lemma D2.5.6 each turn beyond the single $\tau$-turn is a step acting on some $m \prec n_{(0)}$; the candidates are finitely many (names in $N$), so some $n_{(1)} \prec n_{(0)}$ takes infinitely many steps. Iterating produces $n_{(0)} \succ n_{(1)} \succ n_{(2)} \succ \cdots$, all with infinitely many steps; $N$ finite forces $n_{(i)} = n_{(j)}$ for some $i < j$, exhibiting a $\prec$-cycle $C$ every member of which takes infinitely many steps. Each member is present from its (unique within the sequence — no removals occur: O-Remove is an orchestration rule and registrations' inverses are O-Retires, Def. 47) insertion onward, so from some index $T^\flat$ on, all of $C$ are co-present. A member taking a step at $t \ge T^\flat$ is, at $\gamma^t$ or $\gamma^{t+1}$, installed (every lifecycle rule other than L-Begin has premise $\theta \neq \mathsf{Inactive}(-)$, and L-Begin's successor is installed) — at a reachable state where all of $C$ is co-present. That contradicts Lemma D2.5.3. Hence no infinite sequence exists; by (1) the endpoint of a maximal sequence is quiescent. $\square$

**Remark D2.5.7a (relation to Thm. 66's measure).** Within Thm. 66's hypotheses ($\prec$ acyclic), the quantitative bound stands as stated there: $S(n) \le (K+4)(V(n)+1)$ with $V(n) \le 1 + \sum_{m \prec n} S(m)$, closed by the well-founded recursion $B(n) = (K+4)(2 + \sum_{m \prec n} B(m))$, so $\sum_n S(n) \le \sum_n B(n)$ — an explicit rank. Theorem D2.5.7 trades the explicit rank for an infinite-descent argument in exchange for dropping acyclicity; where a rank is wanted in the general case, one is induced a posteriori: order the finitely many maximal sequences' lengths (all finite by the theorem) — but no *uniform* closed-form bound is claimed for cyclic $\prec$, and none is needed below.

### 5.5 Termination transfer

**Theorem D2.5.8 (termination direction).** Let $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ and $\mathbb I \models T^\star$. Then for every script $\rho$ of the §2 language: every fair settle of $\mathbb I$ during $\rho$ terminates, and it returns at a state whose abstraction is quiescent with the matched report. In particular $\mathbb I$ diverges exactly where $\mathbb S$ does — nowhere on this language — and $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho)$ contains a $\mathsf{Diverge}$ outcome iff $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho)$ does (both never).

*Proof.* Fix a fair settle of $\mathbb I$ and suppose its run is infinite. By Lemma D2.5.1 it has infinitely many tagged steps; let $e_j$ denote its prefix up to the $j$-th tagged step. Each $e_j$ is a finite tagged execution, and Theorem D2.4 matches it by a specification execution $d_j$; moreover the matchings can be taken coherent (each $d_{j+1}$ extending $d_j$: the type-F argument of Theorem D2.4 extends the *given* match by one step — this is how the induction there runs — so a single chain $d_1 \sqsubseteq d_2 \sqsubseteq \cdots$ exists). During a settle, the tagged steps are lifecycle-tagged (the script offers no input until the settle returns; orchestration tags cannot occur; CF1's tagging admits no third kind), so $\bigcup_j d_j$ is an infinite lifecycle-only specification sequence from the matched settle-start state — a reachable state of the test language, where $\mathrm{len}(e_n) \le K$ and $N$ finite hold structurally (Theorem D2.5.7's preamble). That contradicts Theorem D2.5.7(2). So the run is finite; being maximal for the settle, its last state $u$ has no enabled offer left un-taken under fairness, and by Theorem D2.4 (type B) every specification-enabled rule at the matched $\gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}A(u)$ is offered at $u$ — so none is enabled at $\gamma$, i.e. $\gamma$ is quiescent by Theorem D2.5.7(1)'s contrapositive; CF6(i) returns the settle there, and the report matches by Theorem D2.4's report clause. For the last claim: $\mathbb S$ yields no $\mathsf{Diverge}$ on any $\rho$ by Theorem D2.5.7, and $\mathbb I$ yields none by the above. $\square$

**Proposition D2.5.9 (what termination-sensitivity adds — the exact statement).** Let $\mathbf{conf}^-$ be Definition 1 with clause 2 (termination agreement) and Definition 2's last clause (fair settling terminates or diverges on the same scripts) deleted, and let $\models^-$ be Definition 5 with its termination requirement deleted. Then: (i) Theorems D2.4 and D2.6's necessity direction survive the deletion verbatim — finite-prefix matching never used the termination clauses. (ii) Sufficiency does **not**: there is a system $\mathbb J \notin \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ but satisfying CF1–CF5 and CF7 with $f^{\mathbb J} = f^{\mathbb S}$, whose settle never returns (it interleaves the matched tagged steps with an infinite untagged stutter after quiescence, violating only CF6(i)); $\mathbb J \models^- T^\star$ and $\mathbb J$'s finite behaviors are exactly $\mathbb S$'s, yet $\mathbb J$ produces no quiescent report at all and $\mathbb J \not\mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$. Dually, a system violating only fairness can starve one of two enabled rules forever, never realizing an offered outcome, and passes every per-run check while its $\mathrm{Out}$-set is a strict subset of $\mathbb S$'s. Hence the termination-and-fairness content of the bisimulation is exactly: (a) CF6 as an audit obligation, and (b) Definition 5's termination clause as the executable judgment — clause (b) is checkable by the suite (case (e) of Theorem D2.3), clause (a) is not, and the two together are what upgrade finite-prefix bisimulation to the termination-sensitive $\mathbf{conf}$. *Proof of (ii):* $\mathbb J$ as described satisfies the finite-prefix clauses by construction (its tagged steps are $\mathbb S$'s); it fails Definition 1's clause 2 on any script whose settle $\mathbb S$ completes (every script, by Theorem D2.5.7), since $\mathbb J$ has a divergent outcome and no $\mathsf{Done}$ outcome there; and it fails no $\models^-$ verdict, as those never await the settle's return. $\square$

## 6. The Main Theorem

**Theorem D2.6 (finite characterization; PAPER_REWRITE Theorem 1, full).** For every clause-factored implementation $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$,

$$\boxed{\;\mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathbb I \models T^\star\;}$$

on the orchestration and component language of §2 and the termination domain of [SZC26] — where, by Theorem D2.5.7, the termination domain now covers the whole language, the acyclicity proviso being discharged rather than assumed. Equivalently, in MASTER's display:

$$\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}} \implies \big(\, \mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S \iff \mathbb I \models T^\star \,\big).$$

*(Vocabulary proviso, from §3.)* The statement is for the certificate-instrumented observation vocabulary; by Proposition D2.3.1's consequences, in the certificate-erased vocabulary both sides must be read on the observation-relevant quotient of the decision tables, and the biconditional persists in that quotiented form because masked entries perturb neither side.

### 6.1 Necessity: $\mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S \implies \mathbb I \models T^\star$

*Proof.* Fix $(\rho_{r,s}, V_{r,s}) \in T^\star$. By Definition 1, $\mathbf{conf}$ supplies an abstraction $A$ under which $\mathbb I$ and $\mathbb S$ are observation-bisimilar, entailing for *every* finite script — in particular for $\rho_{r,s}$ — its three clauses. We derive the two conjuncts of passing (Definition 5).

*(1) Every fair choice is visited and classified.* Definition 5(1) requires the explorer to visit every fair choice up to quiescence, refusal, a repeated normalized state, or the scenario's bound. This is a property of the test *harness*, assumed of the execution of the judgment $\models$ itself (it is what "the complete finite outcome set" means); it is not a property of $\mathbb I$ and nothing here needs proving about $\mathbb I$.

*(2) $V_{r,s}(\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s}))$ holds.* $V_{r,s}$ is, by construction (PAPER_REWRITE §5.2), the predicate "$\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s}) \equiv \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho_{r,s})$, and every $\mathbb I$-run terminates where every $\mathbb S$-run does". We prove outcome-set equality; the termination conjunct is then Definition 1's clause 2 directly.

$\subseteq$: Let $o \in \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s})$, produced by a run $\pi$ under some fair scheduler. **Why the quantification over $\mathbb I$'s runs is covered:** $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}$ quantifies over *all* fair schedulers, whereas the bisimulation clauses of Definition 2 are stated per transition; the bridge is that a bisimulation matches *every* transition $\mathbb I$ actually takes, whichever scheduler chose it — the forward clauses quantify over all $\mathbb I$-transitions from every related pair, not over a preferred run. So induct along $\pi$: at each orchestration input, clause "each orchestration transition of $\mathbb I$ is matched by the corresponding transition or refusal of $\mathbb S$" extends the matched $\mathbb S$-run and matches the refusal log; at each lifecycle transition tagged $(r', n')$, the clause matches it by the $r'$-transition of $\mathbb S$ at the related state with equivalent events and successor abstraction — and equivalent *certificates*, since a certificate is a function of the tag, the normalized window, and the normalized update, all matched (the window by the successor-abstraction relation and Lemma D2.0.6; the update by the matched abstract successor; no CF4 is needed here beyond $\mathbb I$'s certificates being part of its observed word, which $\equiv$ compares); untagged steps contribute nothing. If $\pi$ ends in a completed settle, the clause "quiescent states have equivalent reports" matches $q$; if in a refusal, the refusal clause; divergence cannot occur (Definition 1 clause 2 with Theorem D2.5.7 on the $\mathbb S$ side — and if it did occur it would be matched by the last clause anyway). The assembled $\mathbb S$-run yields $o' \equiv o$.

$\supseteq$: Let $o' \in \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb S}(\rho_{r,s})$. By the converse bisimulation clause ("every orchestration or lifecycle transition enabled by $\mathbb S$ has a correspondingly labeled implementation transition or refusal with an $A$-related successor"), induct along $o'$'s run to build an offered run of $\mathbb I$ with matching labels, events, certificates, and report. It remains to note the offered run is realized in $\mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}$: $\mathrm{Out}$ is the outcome set over all fair schedulers admitted by $\mathbb I$ (§2.4), and a finite offered run is realized by some admitted scheduler that selects exactly its choices and continues fairly afterwards; its settle completes by Definition 1 clause 2. Hence $o' \in \mathrm{Out}_{\mathbb I}(\rho_{r,s})$ up to $\equiv$.

Both inclusions give set equality; $V_{r,s}$ holds. As $(r,s)$ was arbitrary, $\mathbb I \models T^\star$. $\square$

### 6.2 Sufficiency: $\mathbb I \models T^\star \implies \mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$

*Proof.* Define, with $A$ the CF1 abstraction,

$$R_A := \{(u, \gamma) \mid u \in \mathrm{Reach}(\mathbb I),\ \gamma \in \Gamma_{\mathbb S},\ \gamma \simeq \chi^{-1}\cdot A(u) \text{ for the renaming } \chi \text{ accumulated by the matched history of } u\}.$$

We verify the six clauses of Definition 2 (observation bisimulation).

1. *$A(i_0) = s_0$:* CF1's initial-state clause; the empty renaming.
2. *Each orchestration transition of $\mathbb I$ is matched by the corresponding transition or refusal of $\mathbb S$:* Theorem D2.4, absence of type-F deviations, orchestration case (each accepted input is matched with equal certificate and successor relation; each refusal is matched with equal reason — the table equality covers both polarities).
3. *Each lifecycle transition tagged $(r,n)$ is matched by the $r$-transition of $\mathbb S$ at $n$, with equivalent events and successor abstraction:* Theorem D2.4, type-F lifecycle case.
4. *Conversely every enabled $\mathbb S$-transition has a correspondingly labeled implementation transition or refusal with $A$-related successor:* Theorem D2.4, absence of type-B deviations.
5. *Quiescent states have equivalent reports:* Theorem D2.4's report clause together with CF6(i)/(ii) (settle returns exactly at abstract quiescence, so $\mathbb I$'s quiescent-reported states are those whose abstractions are quiescent).
6. *Fair settling terminates or diverges on the same scripts:* Theorem D2.5.8 (both systems terminate on every script of the language).

Clauses 2–4 hold at every $R_A$-related pair, not only along one run, because Theorem D2.4's induction is over *all* matched prefixes and Lemma 55 transports the matching across $\simeq$-related specification states (the up-to technique named in Lemma 1's proof). So $R_A$ is an observation bisimulation for the abstraction $A$; by Definition 1, $\mathbb I \mathrel{\mathbf{conf}} \mathbb S$ (and Lemma 1 re-derives the entailed per-script conditions, in particular outcome equivalence and no-hidden-acceptance). $\square$

Necessity (§6.1) and sufficiency (§6.2) together give the boxed biconditional, and Theorem D2.1 discharges the theorem's applicability to $\mathbb S$ itself (the tables $f_r^{\mathbb S}$ used throughout are those of a member of the class, so "table equality" is a statement between two members of one formalism, not a pun). $\blacksquare$

**Corollary D2.6.1 (the composite scenarios are corollaries, not axioms).** Every observation-level consequence of the [SZC26] metatheory on its stated domains — recovery exactness (Thm. 61/Cor. 62), ordering (Thm. 63), coherence (Thm. 64), progress (Thm. 66, and Theorem D2.5.7 beyond it), confluence (Thm. 73) — holds of every $\mathbb I \in \mathfrak I_{\mathrm{CF}}$ with $\mathbb I \models T^\star$: by Theorem D2.6, $\mathbb I$'s observations equal $\mathbb S$'s, and the metatheorems constrain $\mathbb S$'s. In particular PAPER.md §5.3's M5 chain (Lemmas 68/70/71/72 + Thm. 73(2) + Lemma 56) transfers verbatim: its statement is an observation predicate over scripts, and both sides of Theorem D2.6 are closed under it. $\square$

## 7. Deviations and corrections

### Deviations from MASTER (logged per MASTER's requirement)

- **D-1 (CF6 reading).** §5.1 fixes an interpretive convention for CF6's "settle terminates exactly when a quiescent state is reached" (return *at* abstract quiescence, in both directions, after finitely many untagged steps). No alternative reading supports Lemma D2.5.1; logged as an interpretation.
- **D-2 (intercept order-pattern bound).** MASTER drafts the intercept occurrence as "depth-as-order-pattern up to 2". §1.11(5) factorizes the merge as a lift-iterated binary fold, so the *proofs* need no depth bound; if D1's reachability analysis exhibits interface-reachable merge chains beyond the drafted pattern, the Σ-table must extend the pattern classes and log the deviation — the factorization argument is insensitive to the choice.

All references here are to the seven-clause definition; MASTER's five-clause summary should be read as eliding, not excluding, CF6–CF7.

### Corrections the proofs force on PAPER_REWRITE's skeleton

- **(C1) Lemma 6's reachability step is a genuine gap, now filled.** The skeleton's proof of Lemma 6 asserts "Every resulting atomic-signature occurrence is reachable" in one line. That assertion is exactly the CF1/induction interaction: $A(u)$ of an arbitrary reachable implementation state need not be $\mathbb S$-reachable, and the occurrences are reachable only because the *matched prefix* plants $A(u)$ at ($\simeq$-, $\chi$- image of) an $\mathbb S$-reachable state (Theorem D2.4, "reachability bridge"). The skeleton should cite the minimal-counterexample structure, or fold Lemmas 5–6 into Theorem D2.4's induction as done here.
- **(C2) Lemma 2's CF6 clause is under-proved in the skeleton and its statement can be strengthened.** "Follows from the progress theorem on its stated finite, acyclic domain and is checked directly for canonical quiet states outside that domain" does not cover arbitrary reachable $\prec$-cyclic states, which the sufficiency direction traverses. Theorem D2.5.7 proves progress and termination for *all* reachable states of the test language with no acyclicity hypothesis (via Lemmas D2.5.2–D2.5.4); the skeleton's §4.3/§6 prose and Theorem 1's "termination domain" qualifier should be updated to say the domain is the whole language.
- **(C3) Definition 3 / CF3 must locate the order data.** $\kappa_r(W)$ is a multiset; the accumulator's composition order and iterator position must be assigned to the window's *frame*, consumed by $\mathrm{lift}_r$ (Remark D2.0.5), or transport as stated in the skeleton's Lemma 6 is false for windows agreeing as multisets but differing in order. One clarifying sentence in §4.2 suffices; Theorem D2.2 gives the corrected statement.
- **(C4) CF4 should be split into faithfulness (obligation) and reflection (lemma), and a certificate-adequacy condition added.** As written, CF4 nearly assumes what Lemma 5 proves. The workable reading — instrumentation truthfully and synchronously reports the consulted occurrence and performed update, with $\widehat a$ ranging over all of $\widehat U_r$ including event-and-report-invisible aspects — makes reflection provable (Theorem D2.3) and is *necessary*: Proposition D2.3.1 exhibits masked entries (retirement-unload $\mathsf{provide}^{-1}$ skip) where no event/report-derived certificate can witness the deviation. §4.2's threat item 2 should name this adapter obligation explicitly.
- **(C5) Theorem 1's statement gains a vocabulary proviso.** With certificates: as stated. Without: both $\mathbf{conf}$ and $T^\star$ must be read on the observation-relevant quotient of $\mathrm{Decision}_r$; the biconditional survives (masked deviants conform *and* pass — self-healing), but "passing determines the tables" (Lemma 5) holds only on the quotient. §6.3 should carry the one-sentence proviso of §6 above.
- **(C6) Definition 2's certificate matching should be explicit.** The bisimulation clauses say "equivalent events"; since certificates are letters of $w$ (§2.4), the clause silently includes them — one clarifying clause ("with equal normalized certificates") would remove the ambiguity §6.1 has to argue around.
